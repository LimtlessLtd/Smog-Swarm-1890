- [x] **Infestation core — the map is hostile everywhere, and it can now say so (2026-08-28).** `infestation` appeared nowhere in the code; `vision.md` P1 called it "the largest gap between the vision and the build". New `InfestationManager` implements design_doc.md §2.1: one saved count per hex, `infestation`/`is_cleared`/band all derived (D1, D2), D7's worldgen rings 0/25/50/75/100 out from the player's own hex, D4's breed-and-export pump, and §2.1's Build Rights column on buildings and walls. **The single design call worth reading is where a hex's zombies live (D37).** `Horde` is already a saved `Resource` owned by `HordeManager`, so a per-hex ledger that mirrored horde sizes would be the double-source-of-truth D5 deletes the diffusion PDE to avoid — and staying in sync means chasing four traps this codebase actually has: the combat kill path emits **no signal at all** (`CombatCoordinator` writes `Horde.apply_remaining_hp()` directly), merge and split both emit `horde_size_changed` while conserving the total, `_knock_back()` moves a horde without emitting `horde_moved`, and `HordeManager.load_save_state()` replaces every horde silently. So a hex's `zombie_count` is its stored residents PLUS whatever hordes stand on it, read live through two new `HordeManager` methods. Three §2.1 rules then need no code: killing a horde lowers that hex's infestation (D8's only suppression), a horde crossing a Cleared hex re-infests it while it is there, and export conserves exactly. **Two bounds the spec did not have, both measured rather than guessed.** D7 seeds ring four outward at 100%, which on the real map is **4,666 of 4,692 land hexes** — every one an export candidate on day one, into a manager whose merge check is per co-located pair and whose `get_hordes_at()` is a linear scan. So export is capped at one hex per day, nearest-first, within 8 hexes of a player BUILDING (not of any `is_settlement` hex — Birmingham and London are settlement hexes from the first frame and the player holds neither). Distant Hive Cores still breed and still sit at 100%; their counts are real, they just do not ship (D39). And §2.1's "export down to 75" taken literally ships the whole 25% in one day: measured on the real map that is **37,582 zombies on day one**, out of a Manchester suburb six hexes from a colony holding a Town Hall, a Lumber Yard and one farm. Capping the RATE at 2.5% of capacity per day keeps the mechanism §2.1 specifies and gives **3,758 on day one, 61,459 over 60 days across 7 new hordes** — with the nearest horde still growing past 37,000 if nobody engages it (D38). §2.1 calls the spawn figure "a balancing number, not a design decision"; this is the same kind of number on the other side of the pump, and `scripts/test/diagnose_infestation_pressure.gd` exists so the next person to turn it can see what it does. **Build rights, and what was deliberately not built (D40).** `BuildingManager.get_infestation_placement_error()` and `WallManager._infestation_error()` implement the Build Rights column; "Total Severance", the Contested stockpile split and the Fringe -25% logistics efficiency are absent because **there is no logistics throughput number anywhere in the codebase to reduce** — `SupplyLineCatalog.get_speed_multiplier()` is unit MOVEMENT speed, and `BuildingCatalog` says outright that no throughput system exists — and the split needs per-settlement stockpiles, which `backlog.md` insists is its own PR. Split into its own backlog item rather than half-built. "Defensive Tier" is a new `BuildingDefinition.is_defensive` flag, not a `BuildingCategory` test: the category enum splits its four members across three categories (Watchtower and Garrison HOUSING_CIVIL, Supply Dump INDUSTRY_EXTRACTION deliberately, Search Light the sole DEFENSE_WORKS member) and cannot express walls at all. **`TerritoryController` is left alone (D41)** — it answers "did the player lose a building here to a horde", an event and a boolean, where this answers "how many zombies are here", a population and a ratio. Both class doc comments now say which is which. `BuildingManager`'s header, which explicitly refused to gate placement on `District.is_contested` because that flag defaults TRUE everywhere, is amended rather than left to read as a contradiction. **Ordering is load-bearing and stated:** `InfestationManager` must be a later `Main.tscn` sibling than `BuildingManager`, because its rings are measured from the hex `seed_starting_buildings()` puts down; `BuildingManager` and `WallManager` therefore resolve it with `get_node_or_null` rather than `get_node`. `SaveLoadManager` reached thirteen NodePaths, which CLAUDE.md §1 flags — stated in its header rather than ignored: aggregating every manager IS its single responsibility, so a "narrower collaborator" would be a second aggregator with one more indirection. - [x] **Zombies ignore infrastructure (2026-08-27).** D6, verbatim: "zombies shouldn't travel faster over roads or rails or canals because they can't drive or take the train lol". **The backlog item's own framing was wrong and the fix would have been half-done if followed literally.** It named three neighbour-expansion loops — `find_path()`, `HordeFlowField._build_field()` and `HordeManager._replan_cheap()` — but infrastructure reaches a mover through TWO mechanisms, not one loop-shaped one: `get_step_cost()` decides which way something GOES, `get_movement_speed_multiplier()` decides how fast it MOVES. Enumerating every caller found that `_replan_cheap()` needed **no change at all** (it applies neither modifier — its only logistics use is `is_water_crossing_blocked()`), that `find_path()` is not called for hordes at all since Phase 2b replaced it with the flow field, and that the site nobody had listed — `HordeManager._movement_speed()` — is the one that literally makes zombies travel faster on roads. A horde denied the routing discount alone still sprints down any road its drift crosses. **Bridges are passability, not speed.** The tempting implementation is to pass a null `LogisticsNetwork` for hordes; that is the same object `is_water_crossing_blocked()` consults for bridges, so it would have silently made every river in the game uncrossable for zombies — a much larger change than the one asked for, and one that would have read as terrain bugs rather than a pathfinding regression. Implemented instead as an explicit `uses_infrastructure: bool = true` on `get_step_cost()`, `get_movement_speed_multiplier()` and `find_path()`, with the network still passed. `HordeFlowField` hardcodes false rather than taking the flag as a parameter — it exists only to move hordes, and a parameter would silently corrupt its field cache, whose key is the goal hex alone (two callers wanting different answers for one goal would share the first one's field). **Verified by `scripts/test/verify_horde_infrastructure.gd`** (scene-based, hand-built fixture, same reasoning `verify_gates.gd` records for not generating the real map). Five checks, each asserting the PLAYER's answer changes over the identical fixture so that "horde unaffected" cannot pass vacuously: step cost over a railway is 0.250 for the player and 1.000 for a horde; speed is 4.0x against 1.0x; the player's A* route takes the two-hex railed detour `[(0,0), (1,-1), (1,0)]` while the horde goes direct `[(0,0), (1,0)]`; the flow field's route is byte-identical with a railway and with no network at all; and a bridged water crossing is blocked without the bridge, open with it, routable by a horde, and crossed at terrain speed rather than the bridge's own 1.25x. **One fixture subtlety worth recording:** the routing check first failed with the direct hex left as plain ground, and not because the discount was broken — `find_path()`'s own doc comment already records that its hex-count heuristic is inadmissible once a speed multiplier is in play. A* terminates when it POPS the goal, and an adjacent goal at f = 1.0 pops before the railed detour's first hex at f = 1.25, so the cheaper route is never examined. The direct hex is WETLAND (1.8x) so the detour's entry sorts ahead of it and the search sees both. **Gate:** `check_gdscript` clean (166 .gd, 48 scene/resource), 9/9 verifications in ~46 s, `Main.tscn --headless --quit` boots clean.

- [x] **Fine elevation bake — 30 m elevation in metres, corridor-wide (2026-08-27).** `bake_fine_relief.py` has sampled real elevation at 30 m since the relief bake, and thrown the metres away: its header said so outright, and predicted that a metres channel would be "a small change, not a re-fetch" because the z12 Terrarium tiles stay in `cache/terrarium`. Both halves of that turned out to be true — 26,980 cached z12 tiles, **zero downloads**, 19.6 min of pure CPU for all 3,876 corridor hexes. **What the coarse raster actually held:** `bake_landcover.py` writes `elevation.png` at ~878 m/px but samples it with `step = 4` and a NEAREST-NEIGHBOUR upsample, so the real interval is ~3,510 m — about 3x3 values per hex, and every elevation read anywhere in the game was one of nine numbers. Measured against the new tiles over a 40x40 grid on 24 sampled hexes: **125.5 distinct values per hex against the coarse raster's 8.7, a 14.4x gain** (`scripts/test/verify_fine_elevation.gd`). **Format (D31):** the same Terrarium RGB packing `RealTerrainSampler.sample_at()` and `ReliefImageBuilder._decode_elevation_metres()` already decode, so no third decoder exists anywhere; Terrarium's blue channel (1/256 m) is written as zero, since nothing wants sub-metre precision and it is high-entropy noise no compressor shrinks. Round-trip verified at 0.5 m worst case, which is rounding and nothing else. `encode_png_rgb8()` gained Sub filtering for this — elevation bytes are smooth at 30 m spacing where the two rasters that function was written for (packed classification codes, Terrarium's raw low byte) are not: **106 MB for the corridor against ~800 MB unfiltered**, 26.8 KB/tile. **Reader:** new `FineElevationTiles.gd`, extracted rather than bolted onto `RealTerrainSampler` (already answering biome, feature, coarse elevation and fine land-cover). It carries a **bounded** LRU of 32 tiles ~ 10 MB; these are ~325 KB each, so the unbounded static Dictionary this codebase reaches for by habit would strand ~1.3 GB across the corridor — `RealTerrainSampler._fine_tile_cache` has exactly that shape today. The bound is asserted, not assumed: after 120 distinct hex lookups the cache holds 32. **Origin snapping is load-bearing and was the real trap.** The relief bake snaps every tile origin down to a multiple of the pixel step so all tiles are subsets of ONE global lattice; `RealTerrainSampler._sample_fine()` does NOT snap, and is correct not to, because the land-cover tiles it reads come from `bake_fine_tiles.py`, which does not snap either. Two bakes, two lattices. Copying the nearer-looking existing code would have sampled up to a pixel off. Verified rather than reasoned about: **122 overlapping hex pairs compared at their shared midpoint, 0 disagree, worst delta 0.0 m.** **Terrarium carries bathymetry, which nothing here knew.** The verification's first run failed on -122 m and the assumption under it — "below sea level means a decode bug" — was the thing that was wrong. Confirmed against the source at z12: Liverpool Bay -11.9 m, mid Irish Sea -50.1 m, Thames Estuary -12.0 m, against Ben Nevis 1313.5 m and Manchester 44.4 m. Sea returns real depth, not zero and not "no data". This matters well beyond the test: a canal gradient rule that assumed sea was 0 m would read the shoreline as a legal step, and a line-of-sight ray would pass under the water. Documented on both the reader and the check. **Deliberately NOT done — map generation is unchanged (D32).** It is one line to point `_sample_fine()` at the new tiles and give every existing reader 30 m elevation for free. That line also silently regenerates the map: `RealTerrainSampler.majority_biome()` takes the MAX elevation of a 5x5 grid, and its own comment records that this is only safe because the coarse raster holds 4x4 identical blocks — the max means "some substantial upland part of this hex is above the line" rather than a point maximum, and it says outright that this stops being true if the source gets finer. At 30 m one summit would classify a whole 25-square-mile hex as Level 4 MOUNTAIN, feeding `MountainPassCarver` and every passability decision downstream. `SubHexTerrainQuery.elevation_metres()` is the new sanctioned fine read path (CLAUDE.md §3); `sample()["elevation_m"]` stays coarse. `verify_mountain_ranges` and `verify_elevation` both still pass, which is the evidence that nothing moved. Filed as a `[design]` backlog item because it needs a before/after mountain-hex count and the user's call, not a side effect of a bake. **Known limitation, stated rather than buried:** these tiles are sampled through `geo_projection.CALIBRATION_POINTS`, whose worst residual is 3,328 world units (~6.5 hex radii, at Warwick). The elevation is real and self-consistent with the relief and land-cover bakes, which use the same projection — but it is real elevation for a place slightly beside the one the hex claims to be. Fixing that projection is its own open item and will require re-running this bake. **Third instance of the export defect.** `FineElevationTiles` uses `Image.load()` on a `res://` path for the same reason `ReliefTileView` and `RealTerrainSampler` do — it bypasses the texture importer, which must not re-encode bytes whose exact values are the data. It therefore inherits the same "will not work on export" defect. Not fixed here: repairing one of three readers would leave the other two broken and hide the shared cause. Noted on the existing backlog item so they get fixed together. **Gate:** `check_gdscript.py` clean (167 .gd, 47 scene/resource); `run_verifications.py` **9/9 in ~50 s** with the new check discovered automatically; `Main.tscn --headless --quit` boots clean.

- [x] **Sub-hex elevation relief + sub-hex boundary passability (2026-08-19).** Two granularity fixes plus the visual the first one was failing to deliver, all driven by the user rule now recorded as `CLAUDE.md` §3 ("we need to stop linking things to the hex tile system altogether"). **(1) `ElevationReliefView` rewritten off `HexCell` entirely.** Its first version drew a per-band flat tint, contour lines on hex EDGES, and diagonal hatching over any hex whose `HexCell.is_passable()` was false — the red crosses over whole hexes the user screenshotted. All three described a hex rather than the ground: a band tint shades a plateau and the cliff below it identically, hex-edge contours draw slopes that follow hex geometry instead of terrain, and one hatched hexagon claims a whole 25-square-mile tile is impassable because part of it is. Replaced by a slope-derived **hillshade** built per elevation-raster pixel by a new `ReliefImageBuilder` (pure computation; the view owns only placement/visibility/lifecycle), composited with a per-pixel impassable wash, drawn as ONE bilinear-filtered texture over the raster's own world rect. Resolution is ~11x11 samples per hex against the old 1 — that is the coarse raster's real limit, since the 30 m fine tiles bake biome/`terrain_feature` but deliberately not elevation (see the new open item on a fine elevation bake).   **Pipeline, and the two orderings that had to be measured rather than guessed.** Per class: union the RAW features touching a chunk, THEN simplify the merged blob — simplifying each feature *first* destroys anything near the tolerance, measured directly (a 30m residential polygon simplified at a 30m tolerance vanished; the whole arrangement collapsed from ~89k vertices to 7,129, essentially all of them WATERWAY). Then snap to a precision grid, node the ENTIRE boundary network with one `unary_union`, `polygonize` into faces, label each face by priority, and CDT each face. Building the partition **sequentially** instead (each class differenced against everything already claimed) was tried first and measured: 7 T-junctions on the very first adjacent pair, because where a lower-priority class's boundary meets the INTERIOR of a higher-priority class's edge, the higher-priority side has no vertex there — under Phase 4's vertex displacement a hanging vertex slides off its neighbour's straight edge and opens a visible crack. It also threw a GEOS `TopologyException` on a non-noded intersection. The arrangement construction measures **0 T-junctions**, exact area conservation, and (verified, since the size budget depends on it) **no Steiner points** — GEOS CDT keeps exactly the input boundary vertices.

  **Tolerance chosen against the user's own stated bound, not by eye.** One `SubHexGroundView` rendered cell is `SUB_HEX_GRID_SPAN/_GRID_N` = 1024/11 = 93.1 world units, so "up to same size as current tactical view biome tiles" means ~93 wu.   **Irregular triangle size cap.** Without one the empty-moorland background is a single face spanning the chunk and CDT gives it a 4096-wu edge — a triangle ~40 km across cannot follow terrain, so Phase 4 would interpolate elevation linearly across it. A square subdivision grid would reintroduce exactly the artifact this epic exists to remove, so the cap is a **Voronoi diagram of jittered seeds** (irregular convex cells, no axis-aligned edges), seeded off each chunk's own integer origin so a re-bake is byte-identical. Its cell boundaries join the line network BEFORE `polygonize`, not applied to faces afterwards — cutting a face after the arrangement inserts vertices its neighbour doesn't have, which is T-junctions straight back. Measured cost: +31% triangles, max edge 4096 → 184.9.

  **Two more failure modes surfaced only once the healed cache made chunks denser.** `simplify(preserve_topology=True)` keeps a ring from crossing itself but does not guarantee a valid polygon across thousands of unioned OSM features, and `set_precision` on an invalid input throws `TopologyException: side location conflict` instead of returning — **7 chunks failed outright, two of them Manchester's**; `_snap()` now repairs with `make_valid()` and retries. That was caught only because the verifier counted 110 chunks against the bake's own reported 105 — **worth checking a producer's claimed output count against what is actually on disk, since neither number is wrong on its own.**

  **The in-engine verifier caught a real defect the Python side had missed:** 273 degenerate zero-area triangles (0.06%), every one with a 1.0-wu edge. The bake only rejected *duplicate* vertex indices, but three points can stay distinct as integers and still snap into collinearity on the quantization grid. Now rejected on the quantized cross product; a zero-area triangle draws nothing and yields a degenerate normal once Phase 4 displaces vertices.

  **A second real defect, found only by LOOKING at the mesh** — `scripts/test/preview_terrain_mesh.gd` renders the same world rect twice, raster squares beside vector triangles, and the vector side came back full of holes. Measured: **96.80% area coverage, 93.83% in the densest chunk.** Cause was `MIN_PART_AREA_M2` being applied to arrangement FACES as well as to input features. On a feature that filter is right (skip a 200×200m car park); on a face it is a category error — a face is a region of *space*, so dropping one deletes that ground rather than merging it into a neighbour, and it deletes most where the arrangement is densest, i.e. exactly the cities. Faces are nearly free to keep: their vertices are already in the noded line network whether or not the face is triangulated, so removing the filter cost **+35% triangles but only +7.6% vertices**. - [x] **Biome shapes conditioned at bake: no more shards or slivers (2026-08-19).** "can we look at the one about smoothing the biomes out so they dont have such jagged angles and tiny slivers of land" (user). Whole map re-baked.

  **The jaggedness was not noisy source data — it was `SIMPLIFY_TOLERANCE_M` being LARGER than the features it was applied to.** British farmland and woodland arrive from OSM as thousands of individual field and copse polygons roughly 100–300 m across, i.e. at or below the 300 m tolerance. Douglas-Peucker with `preserve_topology=True` refuses to delete a ring outright, so instead of vanishing each one collapses to the three or four extreme points a ring cannot go below — a spike. The shard texture covering every rendered chunk was **one triangular splinter per real field**. The tolerance had been chosen (correctly, for what it was measured against) from p95 EDGE LENGTH, which says nothing about whether the shapes survive.

  **Fix is four stages around the existing simplify, and the ORDER is the whole point.** `_despeckle()` runs BEFORE it: a closing (`FIELD_WELD_M = 90`) welds the field mosaic across the lanes, hedge lines and hairline no-data slits that separate adjacent features in OSM, after which the 300 m tolerance applies to a REGION's outer boundary — which is what a 300 m tolerance is the right size for — and the interior boundaries it used to shred are gone rather than simplified; then an opening (`SLIVER_OPEN_M = 40`) erases what the weld could not join. `_smooth()` runs AFTER it, Chaikin corner-cutting on each ring, cyclic so no ring keeps one hard corner at whichever vertex OSM happened to list first. **The first version smoothed before the simplify and it was pointless work** — DP then picked a subset of the rounded vertices and handed back the same angular polyline. `_drop_small()` now filters HOLES as well as parts: a 150 m gap left inside a wood is not a clearing at this scale, and because faces are labelled by containment it came out as a speck of DEFAULT_CLASS moorland sitting inside the wood.

  **The priority order decides how far each class may be welded, and that is not a detail.** A weld can only claim ground no HIGHER-priority class covers, because faces are still labelled by `PRIORITY` afterwards — so FARMLAND, last in the list, can weld generously and take nothing but `DEFAULT_CLASS`, which is the correct owner of the lane between two fields anyway (it means "no OSM polygon covers this", not "surveyed as moor"). A weld near the TOP of the list takes ground from the real classes below it: measured on chunk 29,15, welding WOODLAND at 100 m grew it from **6.81% of the chunk to 10.83%**, all of it out of farmland, because a 150 m gap between two copses is usually a field and not more wood. Hence `CONSERVATIVE_CLASSES` (WOODLAND/HEATHLAND/WETLAND/URBAN/INDUSTRIAL) at `CONSERVATIVE_FRACTION = 0.45`. The reduced opening goes with it — at the full 40 m a copse under 80 m across is erased outright, and chunk 29,15 LOST woodland (6.81% → 5.95%) in the version that did that.

  **`_absorb_unclaimed()` handles what conditioning structurally cannot reach.** Small isolated pockets of ground no class claims are not part of any class's geometry — they are what is LEFT once every class has been welded — so no per-class operation can touch them. On chunk 33,27 (London) they were the grey city's remaining olive confetti: 300 m pockets of "moorland" that are really street junctions nobody drew a landuse polygon over. Each pocket under `DEFAULT_ABSORB_AREA_M2` (0.25 km²) goes to the class it shares the most BOUNDARY LENGTH with — not nearest centre, not largest neighbour; a pocket wedged between a city and a wood belongs to whichever actually wraps it. Real moorland is safe for the opposite reason: on an actual moor the unclaimed region is one connected part covering most of the chunk (measured 89% on chunk 25,9), far above the threshold.

  **WATERWAY is exempt from every stage, and separately excluded as an absorption target.** Its centreline is already simplified and Chaikin-smoothed before buffering and the ribbon is only 4.62 wu wide, so an erosion of any useful size breaks it into pieces. The absorption exemption was found by measuring, not predicted: a pocket inside a meander is bounded mostly by river, so it won shared-length every time and the river silently fattened — **4.77% → 5.37% of chunk 33,27** before the exemption, back to exactly 4.77% after. That is the same widening `WATERWAY_LINE_TOLERANCE_M` records, and river width is load-bearing for Bridge-mandatory crossing rather than cosmetic.

  **Measured on chunk 29,15 (Pennine edge), before → after:** 105,503 → 61,991 triangles and 929 → 546 KB, i.e. the conditioning makes the map **smaller** as well as cleaner, because welding deletes far more interior boundary than Chaikin's ring-doubling adds. Class shares moved as the reasoning predicts: MOORLAND 26.64% → 9.62% and FARMLAND 40.15% → 57.43% (default-class filler becoming the farmland it always was), while the real classes barely moved — HEATHLAND 22.83% → 21.05%, WOODLAND 6.81% → 8.16%. **That invariant is the check worth repeating on any future change here: if a conditioning pass moves a REAL class's share much, it is taking land, not tidying it.**

  **URBAN and INDUSTRIAL were exempted from the corner-rounding at first, and the render disproved the argument.** The exemption's reasoning was that a city block and a mill yard are built straight, so rounding them trades one wrong shape for another. But the straight edges it preserved are not the real ones: a village's footprint is a small polygon, so the 300 m simplify turns it into the same spike it turns a field into, and the exemption only kept those spikes sharp. Smoothed, chunk 30,22's villages read as settlements and URBAN's share moves **8.32% → 7.98%** — rounding a corner costs only the corner. At 5 km per macro hex nothing resolves an individual block anyway, so there was no real straightness being defended. The constant is gone rather than left empty.

  **Then the render showed a SECOND source of jagged angles, in the renderer rather than the bake, and this change is what made it visible.** `TerrainMeshView` resolved soil at each triangle's CENTROID and drew the whole triangle in that variant's SVG (`moorland_poor` vs `moorland_desolate` and the farmland equivalents — only those two biomes carry soil art). That was sound while a triangle was smaller than the thing it sampled; welding the field mosaic took the median triangle up past `TEXTURE_WORLD_SIZE` (93 wu), and one sample stopped being able to speak for the area it covered. MOORLAND started drawing large angular wedges of one variant against another with the seams following **triangulation rather than ground** — the same class of defect as the shards, just moved one layer up, and the reason the first in-engine render of the new bake looked worse than the flat-colour preview of the same chunk. New `_add_soil_crossfade()` redraws a triangle in each variant its VERTICES resolve to that the centroid did not, opaque at the vertices holding that soil and transparent at the others, over the opaque base — reusing `_build_surface()`'s existing `alphas` path, the same mechanism `TerrainBoundaryBlend` already uses. **Continuity across a shared edge is exact and provably so, because soil is sampled per VERTEX and adjacent triangles share vertices**: where T1's base is POOR and T2's is DESOLATE, along their shared edge T1 draws `mix(POOR, DESOLATE, t)` and T2 draws `mix(DESOLATE, POOR, 1-t)` — the same colour, with nothing depending on the two triangles agreeing about their bases. The bases must stay OPAQUE for that to hold: N surfaces whose alphas sum to 1 composite to background showing through (two at 0.5 leave 25%), so this is an overlay on a solid surface, not a partition of one. `TerrainVisuals.varies_by_soil()` was added rather than restating the texture-key match statement in the renderer. Known and left: a triangle whose three vertices hold three different ratings composites two overlays and their order then decides the interior mix — sorted keys make that stable rather than correct, and with three ratings meeting inside one triangle there is no single right answer.

- [x] **Two independent bugs, both fixed.** User: "units still cannot move across hex borders" (Debugger showed `Trying to assign an array of type 'Array' to a variable of type 'Array[Dictionary]'` at `UnitOrderController.gd:305`, `Errors (1326)` and climbing).

  1. **Untyped-literal-in-ternary crash.** Both `_advance_toward()` obstacle-list sites read `var obstacles: Array[Dictionary] = [] if <cond> else _gather_obstacles(...)`. GDScript does not push a variable's declared type down into a ternary's branches — the `[]` literal stays a plain untyped `Array` object at runtime regardless of the annotation on the left, so `MovementStepper.advance_toward_hex()`'s typed `Array[Dictionary]` parameter rejected it the moment a unit's stuck-detector went true. - [x] **THE ACTUAL ROOT CAUSE: a gate only worked if it happened to straddle the hex-centre-to-hex-centre line (fixed 2026-08-20).** User had to push back three times ("still the same issue", "DO NOT TELL ME THE PATHING IS CORRECT ITS NOT", "start diagnosing this properly rather than lying") before this was found. Everything above and below it in this entry was a real defect but **none of them was the reported bug** — recorded here as a process failure worth not repeating.

  **The mechanism.** `WallManager.get_blocking_segment()` tested the straight `from_world`→`to_world` line, which for both real callers (`HexPathfinder.find_path()`'s neighbour expansion and `UnitOrderController._blocked_by_wall()`) is hex centre to hex centre. But a unit does NOT walk that line when a gate is present: `UnitOrderController._crossing_offset()` aims it at the gate via `get_gate_crossing_offset()`. So the routing layer was testing a line the mover would never travel. Any solid wall piece sitting on the centre line refused the edge outright, no matter how many gates the player had built on it — the gate was invisible to routing unless it happened to land on that exact line.

  **Measured on the user's own save**, loaded through the real `Main.tscn` + `SaveLoadManager` (`scripts/test/diagnose_route_failure.gd`), per outgoing edge of the unit's hex: exactly one direction was terrain-passable and water-free — `(80,118)`, URBAN/CANAL — it had a gate on it, and it was still refused by a single solid piece (id=74) on the centre line. All six edges had gates. **BFS from the unit's hex reached exactly 1 hex: itself.** The unit was sealed in by its own gated wall.

  **Fix, in `get_blocking_segment()`:** when `ignore_gates` is true and a usable gate stands on this edge, test the line the mover will actually walk (`from_world` → the gate) instead of the centre line. Both real callers go through this one function, so they keep asking the identical question — the live-lock `_blocked_by_wall()`'s own doc comment warns about needs them to agree, and re-aiming here moves both at once. Hordes are untouched (`ignore_gates` is false for them), verified: `verify_gates.gd` still reports horde route 0 hexes and all 6 boundary edges blocked to a horde, unit route 7 hexes.

  **Second, latent bug fixed alongside it, since the first fix would have amplified it:** `get_gate_crossing_offset()` scanned `get_segments_at()`, which returns segments on ALL six of a hex's edges, and returned the nearest gate to the boundary — so it could aim a mover at a gate standing on a completely different side of the hex, and (after the re-aim above) would have opened an edge with no gate on it at all. Now bounded to `_GATE_ON_EDGE_RADIUS` (half an edge length; a pointy-top hex's side equals its circumradius), so only a gate genuinely on THIS edge answers for it.

  **Result on the reported save: reachable hexes went 1 → 3,035**, and `find_path()` across 77 hexes returns an 82-hex route.
