## 🔧 Technical Summary
Godot 4.x/GDScript RTS on a continuous axial hex map of real UK+Ireland geography (Natural Earth coastline, ~25 sq mi/hex, ~4,692 land hexes). Terrain is real data baked offline by `tools/geo_bake/`: land-cover comes from OpenStreetMap vector geometry (Geofabrik `.osm.pbf` extracts of GB + Ireland) and is shipped as a **continuous triangle mesh** — the real polygon boundaries, not a raster and not hex tiles — while elevation stays raster from AWS/Mapzen Terrain Tiles. The mesh covers the whole landmass, not just the old England corridor. Rendering reads the mesh; the 30m mechanical queries still read the older raster, and closing that gap is epic phase 2. A two-tier camera (Strategic hex-icon / Tactical true-scale, `HexCoord.HEX_SIZE` = 512 world units/hex) drives everything from country-scale strategy to individual soldiers. **Phases 1 → 2.12, 4, 5 (core), 6, and 6.3 are built and playable end to end** — full economy, building tree, tech tree, 18-unit roster with combat/morale/veterancy, horde AI, walls/ZoC/supply lines, Fog of War, Day/Night, save/load, and a complete code-drawn HUD. **Not built:** the sewer/underground layer (Phase 3), late-game reclamation and the campaign (Phase 5.11, Phase 7). ## 📖 The Setting
January 1890. Something came down over the Pennines in the night, and within a week the dead of Manchester were walking. The city sealed itself hard — gaslit checkpoints, a militia raised from constables and navvies, whatever the Industrial Revolution could throw up in a fortnight — and held. Beyond the wire, the rest of Britain went dark: no telegraph, no trains, no light after sundown, just the slow drift of the hungry across a country that used to be the workshop of the world. You inherit that quarantine: one lit town in an island of soot and silence, armed with nothing but cast iron, coal smoke, and Victorian grit — no borrowed future technology, just the Industrial Revolution pushed as far as it will go. South of you, Queen Victoria and the Imperial Cabinet are sealed in a bunker under the Tower of London, and nobody above ground knows if they're still alive. Scattered through the wreckage are stranger traces still — wrecked observatories, craters that don't match any war, rumours of things that didn't fall from the sky by accident. Your job isn't rescue. It's to hold the light, push roads and rail south hex by hex, and find out — expedition by expedition — whether Manchester's quarantine was the last sane decision the old world made, or the first sign that whatever did this was already here.

## 🏛️ Project Architecture & Design Overview
- **Core Loop:** Continuous persistent expansion across Victorian Britain hex sectors. Build defenses, automate patrols, manage daily resource upkeeps (Food/Energy/Gunpowder), and protect supply lines while working in the background.
- **Aesthetic Direction:** Grounded late-18th/19th-century Industrial Revolution (brick, cast iron, coal smoke, authentic Victorian architecture)—explicitly **no** retro-futuristic steampunk tropes, including top-tier "mechanized" units (heavy steam engineering, never a humanoid battle-mech).
- **Dual Perspective Engine:** Camera toggle between Top-Down Orthographic and 2.5D Isometric.
- **Multi-Layer Macro-Hex System:** Each hex = a ~25 sq mi macro-region. Cities scale across multiple macro-tiles — Manchester spans 4 hexes, Greater London spans 12. Internal district partitioning and subterranean layers (Sewers/Underground) planned.
- **Time Controls & Day/Night Cycle:** Global `TickManager` supporting `0x`/`1x`/`2x`/`3x`/`5x` speeds. 40-minute real-time day/night cycle at 1x.
- **Accessibility:** Every color-coded piece of state (biome, ZoC, fog-of-war, building category, alerts) is paired with a distinct shape/icon, never color alone.
- **World State Framing:** Outside player-held ground, 1890 Britain is dark and roamed by hordes with no fixed home. Player settlements are the only lit, defended points on the map. Arc runs from defensive "hold the light" early-game toward reclaiming and re-lighting the country late-game (Phase 5.11, Phase 7.4/7.5).

## 🧩 Known Architecture Constraint: Hex-Grid Over-Coupling
> One hex is ~25 sq. miles — most fields below are still a single scalar value for that entire area, which doesn't match real terrain. Biomes/elevation and wall geometry are already real sub-hex resolution (closed 2026-08-10 and earlier). Still hex-locked today:
- **Supply lines** (`SupplyLineSegment.hex_a`/`hex_b`) — whole-edge only, the same shape walls used to have before their rework.
- **Zone of Control, Noise, Fog of War** (`LogisticsNetwork`, `NoiseManager`, `FogOfWarManager`) — flat per-hex `Dictionary` state, no gradient across the hex's own area.
- **Districts** (`District.gd`/`DistrictPartitioner.gd`) — fixed categorical template per hex (always exactly Urban Center + Industrial Estate + Uncleared Wilderness for a settlement hex), no real position/shape/geography.

Revisit if/when a dedicated sub-hex spatial partition gets designed for these — Districts' `Array[District]` per hex is the closest existing seam to extend, though terrain's own fix used a sampled raster instead of a partition since it's read-heavy/write-never data, not the same shape as ZoC/Districts' write-heavy contested-flag-flips-at-runtime need.

**Also disclosed:** the full map span (~214,600×136,700 world units) is past the ~100,000-unit range where single-precision float jitter typically starts (`HexCoord.HEX_SIZE`'s doc comment) — no jitter observed live so far; a floating-origin camera rebase is the real fix if it ever surfaces.

### 🗺️ Sub-Hex Mechanical Layer (new epic, 2026-08-16 — supersedes the old Biomes item above)
User directive: hex-tiles are for visibility/aesthetics ONLY — each displays whichever biome is most common within it (majority-voted from many sub-hex samples, exactly as `HexCell.biome_type` already works today), but every MECHANIC (movement, resource extraction, building placement, vision/LoS, Zone of Control, horde AI) moves onto a real, persistent, **30m-resolution sub-hex grid** underneath. Aim: a much larger-scale "They Are Billions"-style game across Britain and the British Isles, 1890s, with genuine zoomed-in play (individual units/people/buildings), while the map itself stays enormous (playable corridor alone is 3,876 macro-hexes, ~5km each).

Full reasoning, the confirmed architecture, and the two "picked the wrong default, corrected after user pushback" lessons from how this got decided all live in the **`sub-hex-mechanical-layer-epic`** Claude memory file — read that before touching any of these phases, don't re-derive it from scratch. Short version of the confirmed architecture (none of this should be re-litigated without new information):
- Rendering and mechanics are explicitly **decoupled** — fine visual zoom doesn't require the mechanics grid to match rendering resolution (units/buildings already have continuous, non-grid-snapped positions; terrain detail is rendered from real vector geometry that knows nothing about hexes at all).
- 30m mechanics tiles, confirmed deliberately after being shown the naive-dense-storage cost (~430M cells for the corridor) and choosing to commit anyway once shown the architecture that makes it tractable — **not** a compromise/scaled-down number.
- **Sparse, not dense, storage** — on-demand/cached raster lookups for the 99%+ of cells nothing ever mutates (extends `RealTerrainSampler`'s existing pattern); only real gameplay mutations get persistent state, matching `ReclamationManager._drained_hexes`'s existing sparse-`Dictionary` pattern.
- **Hierarchical (HPA*-style) pathfinding** for long-range routing — an abstract per-macro-hex-cluster graph for the long search, refined to real 30m steps only within traversed clusters. Chosen because the user explicitly wants full sub-hex resolution even for long-range routing, and a flat full-resolution search is untenable given `HordeManager`'s own existing "hundreds-to-thousands of hordes" performance comments at today's far coarser resolution.
- **Regional flow-field movement for hordes** specifically (not per-horde A*) — one shared cost-field per active goal/region.
- Real 30m fidelity needs the bake pipeline rebaked at ~12x more pixels per tile than today's ~104m/pixel fine tiles — its own deferred sub-phase, not bundled into the data-model work.

**Phases** (each its own reviewed PR, same one-item-at-a-time discipline as everything else in this file):
- [x] **1a: sparse sub-hex terrain query layer (2026-08-16).** New `HexCoord.gd` sub-hex coordinate math (`SUB_HEX_CELL_SIZE_METERS = 30.0`, `world_to_sub_hex()`/`sub_hex_to_world()`/`sub_hex_index_within()`) and new `SubHexTerrainQuery.gd` (cached, on-demand, reuses `RealTerrainSampler.sample_at_hex()` — a new public method extracted from `sample_grid()`'s own fine-tile-preferring logic — against today's existing bake data, no bake changes yet). `SubHexGroundView` repointed to it as the correctness proof. **Verified, not assumed, that this isn't byte-identical to the old direct-sampling path** (documented in `SubHexGroundView._sample_render_grid()`'s own comment): `SubHexTerrainQuery` snaps every query to its 30m cell's center (correct for a real mechanical grid — nearby positions in one cell must agree), which for a hex WITH a baked fine tile only ever recovers real data at edge samples that used to return empty (strict improvement), but for the ~99% of the corridor WITHOUT one yet, can occasionally cross into a neighboring coarse pixel near a boundary and show an adjacent biome classification (~5% of one test hex's render-grid samples) — a real, small, edge-of-hex-scale rendering difference, not a crash, and moot once Phase 1b rebakes real 30m data. No mechanic (movement/extraction/vision/ZoC/horde AI) reads any of this yet — all still on `HexCell.biome_type` exactly as before.
- [x] **1b: rebake fine-tile pipeline at real 30m-equivalent resolution, full 3,876-hex playable corridor (2026-08-16).** `tools/geo_bake/bake_fine_tiles.py` gained a `--corridor` mode (reuses `bake_landcover.py`'s `CORRIDOR_Q`/`CORRIDOR_R` and, deliberately, its `lonlat_bbox_from_calibration_points()` bbox verbatim rather than deriving one from the hex set — confirmed this makes `fetch_bbox_tiled()`'s tile cache keys land exactly on the 113 tiles/~1.86M OSM elements the coarse whole-corridor bake had already cached, so the corridor-wide fine bake fetched **zero new Overpass tiles**, purely local CPU rasterization) and `FINE_TILE_PIXELS` raised 96→333 (matches `HexCoord.SUB_HEX_GRID_N` from 1a exactly — one baked pixel per 30m mechanical sub-cell, not an approximation). Added a spatial feature index (`_FeatureIndex`, bucketed by projected world-space bbox) — the old per-hex `_feature_might_touch()` linear-scanned the entire fetched feature list per hex, intractable at 3,876 hexes against ~1.6M ring features; bucketing made each hex's query touch only nearby buckets. Also added per-hex resumability (skips existing tiles unless `--force`). Measured: dense-urban worst case (100-hex Manchester cluster) rasterized at ~2s/hex after one-time ~90-150s setup (JSON load + index build); full corridor run completed in 1,430s (~24min), wrote 3,876 tiles (26MB total), no crashes, no zero-byte files. Verified via `--headless scenes/main/Main.tscn --quit` (exit 0, no errors — only the pre-existing "not export-safe" `Image.load()` warning, now firing more often since coverage is corridor-wide instead of a 37-hex demo). `RealTerrainSampler.WORLD_UNITS_PER_PIXEL_FINE` updated to `1024.0/333.0` to match. Every corridor hex now has genuine (not aliased) 30m fidelity where a fine tile exists — `SubHexTerrainQuery`'s Phase 1a-documented cell-center-snap aliasing only still applies to hexes outside the corridor (Wales/Scotland/Ireland, hard-gated, unreached).

### 🗺️ Real-Geography Vector Terrain (new epic, 2026-08-17 — supersedes "Seamless biome blending across hex boundaries")

User complaint that opened this: the existing blending "is not seamless at all, just has various gradients of two biomes but they're still **obviously squares**," and separately "the hex tile cutting off the square biome tiles as they didn't tesselate with a hexagon but triangles do." User's own proposed direction: "discreet polygons / triangles of differing sizes (mostly smaller but also up to same size as current tactical view biome tiles)."

**Root cause, diagnosed before planning anything.** The squares are not a rendering choice and no shader can remove them. The pipeline is: real OSM vector rings → `bake_landcover.py` rasterizes them to a pixel grid → `RealTerrainSampler` samples pixels → `SubHexGroundView` draws one axis-aligned quad per sample. Rasterization is the lossy step, and it happens at bake time — by the time anything renders, the real boundary is already gone. `SubHexGroundView`'s own doc comment had already conceded the second half of the complaint ("A SQUARE sample/render grid can never exactly match a HEXAGONAL footprint"). `BritishGeographyData._LAND_RLE` is the same failure at larger scale: the coastline, the most visible boundary in the game, is quantized to whole hexes.

**A first plan was written and rejected**: subdividing each hex into 6 equilateral triangles, recursively split, driven by sampling the existing rasters. The user redirected — "no it shouldn't be based on the hex tile it should be based on the real GB + Ireland open source data." That rejection was correct on a point the plan had missed: triangulating the *raster* would have inherited every bit of the rasterization damage that causes the squares in the first place. The mesh has to come from the vector source. The hex grid is demoted to purely logical/gameplay.

**Four decisions settled by the user, not assumed:**
- **Elevation:** true vertex-displaced relief (the user chose this over the recommended hillshade-tint-only option).
- **Texture blending:** shader-based multi-texture weighted blend, preserving the hand-drawn SVG art through transitions.
- **Triangulation:** offline bake + runtime patch — Python ships triangle data, and only the affected local region is re-triangulated in-engine when `SubHexTerrainOverride` writes. Forced by the engine: **Godot has no native constrained Delaunay**.
- **Mechanics:** rendering AND mechanics both go fully vector (the user chose this over the recommended "keep hot paths on O(1) raster pixel lookups", accepting the higher-risk single-representation option).

**Elevation deliberately stays raster.** A DEM is a continuous field sampled on a grid at source (Terrarium tiles are raster), so rasterizing it loses nothing — only *categorical boundary* data suffers. HIGHLAND/ESCARPMENT stay derived from that raster too: neither is an OSM class, both come from `bake_landcover.py`'s elevation-threshold override pass, so they are applied on top of the mesh's class rather than stored in it.

**Phases** (each its own reviewed PR, same one-item-at-a-time discipline as the Sub-Hex epic):
- [x] **1: vector land-cover bake — inland corridor (2026-08-17).** New `tools/geo_bake/bake_vector_landcover.py` + `vector_mesh_format.py` (the `.tmesh` wire format) + `scripts/world/TerrainMeshChunkData.gd` (in-engine reader) + `scripts/test/verify_terrain_mesh.gd` (headless cross-language verification) + `scripts/test/preview_terrain_mesh.gd` (renders raster squares beside vector triangles for the same world rect; nothing in the game loads it). Consumes the **already-cached** 2.5 GB / 113 tiles of Overpass JSON — zero new network I/O — and reuses `bake_landcover.py`'s `_LANDUSE_MAP`/`_NATURAL_MAP`/`_BIOME_CODE` by import rather than copying the tables, so the vector mesh and the existing rasters cannot disagree about what a code means. `RealTerrainSampler` gained public `biome_from_code()`/`feature_from_code()` for the same reason (`_BIOME_BY_CODE` is private, and CLAUDE.md forbids reading another class's `_`-prefixed field); the bounds-guarded expression they replace was triplicated inline.
  **Pipeline, and the two orderings that had to be measured rather than guessed.** Per class: union the RAW features touching a chunk, THEN simplify the merged blob — simplifying each feature *first* destroys anything near the tolerance, measured directly (a 30m residential polygon simplified at a 30m tolerance vanished; the whole arrangement collapsed from ~89k vertices to 7,129, essentially all of them WATERWAY). Then snap to a precision grid, node the ENTIRE boundary network with one `unary_union`, `polygonize` into faces, label each face by priority, and CDT each face. Building the partition **sequentially** instead (each class differenced against everything already claimed) was tried first and measured: 7 T-junctions on the very first adjacent pair, because where a lower-priority class's boundary meets the INTERIOR of a higher-priority class's edge, the higher-priority side has no vertex there — under Phase 4's vertex displacement a hanging vertex slides off its neighbour's straight edge and opens a visible crack. It also threw a GEOS `TopologyException` on a non-noded intersection. The arrangement construction measures **0 T-junctions**, exact area conservation, and (verified, since the size budget depends on it) **no Steiner points** — GEOS CDT keeps exactly the input boundary vertices.
  **Tolerance chosen against the user's own stated bound, not by eye.** One `SubHexGroundView` rendered cell is `SUB_HEX_GRID_SPAN/_GRID_N` = 1024/11 = 93.1 world units, so "up to same size as current tactical view biome tiles" means ~93 wu. Swept 60/150/300/600 real m: **300 m** lands p95 edge length at 93.6 wu — at the bound, with 95% smaller, which is the "mostly smaller" the user asked for. 60m gave 7,587 triangles/hex (31× today's 242) for detail far below the bound; 600m gave a p95 of 184.7, double it.
  **Irregular triangle size cap.** Without one the empty-moorland background is a single face spanning the chunk and CDT gives it a 4096-wu edge — a triangle ~40 km across cannot follow terrain, so Phase 4 would interpolate elevation linearly across it. A square subdivision grid would reintroduce exactly the artifact this epic exists to remove, so the cap is a **Voronoi diagram of jittered seeds** (irregular convex cells, no axis-aligned edges), seeded off each chunk's own integer origin so a re-bake is byte-identical. Its cell boundaries join the line network BEFORE `polygonize`, not applied to faces afterwards — cutting a face after the arrangement inserts vertices its neighbour doesn't have, which is T-junctions straight back. Measured cost: +31% triangles, max edge 4096 → 184.9.
  **WATERWAY outranks the built environment here, the OPPOSITE of the raster bake's rule** that a waterway "NEVER overwrites any real polygon-derived classification, full stop." That reversal is resolution-bound, not a disagreement: the raster rule exists because at 878m/pixel a river's rasterization buffer was a ~527m smear that measurably tripled water around Manchester (22.9% → 28.9% of nearby pixels). This bake carries a river as real geometry at its own width, so "a river genuinely running through a city centre should read as a visible ribbon of water through the urban texture" — the thing that comment records wanting and could not have — is now simply true. River width is floored at 1.5 sub-cells because WATERWAY is impassable-without-a-Bridge (`HexPathfinder.is_water_crossing_blocked()`): a ribbon thinner than one 30m sub-cell could fall between sampled cells and silently reopen exactly the crossings that rule closes.
  **Measured, the whole cached corridor — 104 chunks, Manchester to London, 25.4 min:** 2,923,787 triangles / 1,474,933 vertices / **25.1 MB**, 10.1 KB/hex, 1,141 tris/hex against `SubHexGroundView`'s 242 today, worst chunk 35,937 vertices (a 1.8× margin under the format's uint16 index limit). Extrapolates to **~46 MB for the entire ~4,692-hex GB+Ireland landmass** — against ~300 MB for equivalent raster coverage, and against the 42 MB of fine rasters that today cover only the corridor. Edge length over all 8.77M edges: median **25.1 wu**, p95 111.7, max 191.6, with **91.7% under the 93.1 wu render-cell bound** — "mostly smaller, up to the same size" holds, though the corridor-wide p95 sits above the bound where the 15-chunk Manchester sample's was 93.6, because rural faces are larger than urban ones. Per-chunk bake time started at 156s and came down to 38s cold / **8.1s warm** with byte-identical output, once the tile cache was found to be thrashing (capacity 3 against the 5 tiles a Manchester chunk overlaps, fetched *inside* the per-class loop, charging 19.7s of tile parsing 7 times over — 136s of the 156s).
  **The flat-khaki rectangles were a POISONED CACHE, not a corridor edge (found 2026-08-18).** 22 of the 113 cached Overpass tiles were 16 bytes — `{"elements": []}`, written by `fetch_overpass.fetch_tile()`'s own failure path, which cached a network error as a successful empty result. The cache-hit path then returned it forever: the tile was permanently poisoned and no re-run could heal it. Among them were **south Manchester, Liverpool, Oxford, the Chilterns and the Peak District**, each baking into a 40×40 km rectangle of default MOORLAND. That trade was survivable when the rasters were the product and the cost was a slightly wrong pixel; the vector mesh IS the terrain, so it is not.
  Two fixes, both in `fetch_overpass.py`. **A failed tile is never written** — not writing is what "resumable" has to mean for a fetch that can fail. And **failure subdivides**: the 504s were not transient, they were the public instance refusing an over-large query, so retrying the same 0.5° bbox over Manchester failed identically every time; quartering it down to a 0.0625° floor is the lever that works. The floor started at 0.125 and had to come down — Liverpool still 504'd at 0.15°, one step short. Re-fetching healed **19 of 22** (the 3 left are North Sea, and are now simply absent rather than poisoned, so a later run retries them). Cache 113 → 151 tiles.
  A tile that succeeded only after splitting has no cache entry of its own, so `fetch_tile_subdivided()` checks for its quadrants before re-requesting the parent — otherwise every later run pays a guaranteed ~15s of retries and backoff per healed tile before reaching data it already has.
  **After healing, every remaining featureless chunk is at x 32–35 / y 16–22 — the north-east corner, off the Yorkshire and Lincolnshire coast, outside the fetched extent entirely.** The flat rectangles that were scattered through the middle of the corridor are gone. What is left is a real extent limit, which is 1c's job.
  **Two more failure modes surfaced only once the healed cache made chunks denser.** `simplify(preserve_topology=True)` keeps a ring from crossing itself but does not guarantee a valid polygon across thousands of unioned OSM features, and `set_precision` on an invalid input throws `TopologyException: side location conflict` instead of returning — **7 chunks failed outright, two of them Manchester's**; `_snap()` now repairs with `make_valid()` and retries. And a failed chunk left the PREVIOUS run's file in place, so the output directory silently mixed generations: 5 files on disk that no run had written. `_discard_stale()` deletes them now. That was caught only because the verifier counted 110 chunks against the bake's own reported 105 — **worth checking a producer's claimed output count against what is actually on disk, since neither number is wrong on its own.**
  **The in-engine verifier caught a real defect the Python side had missed:** 273 degenerate zero-area triangles (0.06%), every one with a 1.0-wu edge. The bake only rejected *duplicate* vertex indices, but three points can stay distinct as integers and still snap into collinearity on the quantization grid. Now rejected on the quantized cross product; a zero-area triangle draws nothing and yields a degenerate normal once Phase 4 displaces vertices.
  **A second real defect, found only by LOOKING at the mesh** — `scripts/test/preview_terrain_mesh.gd` renders the same world rect twice, raster squares beside vector triangles, and the vector side came back full of holes. Measured: **96.80% area coverage, 93.83% in the densest chunk.** Cause was `MIN_PART_AREA_M2` being applied to arrangement FACES as well as to input features. On a feature that filter is right (skip a 200×200m car park); on a face it is a category error — a face is a region of *space*, so dropping one deletes that ground rather than merging it into a neighbour, and it deletes most where the arrangement is densest, i.e. exactly the cities. Faces are nearly free to keep: their vertices are already in the noded line network whether or not the face is triangulated, so removing the filter cost **+35% triangles but only +7.6% vertices**. The verifier now asserts each chunk's triangle areas sum to its box area (±0.1%), which fails on overlap as well as holes. Neither the round-trip check, the T-junction check, nor the degeneracy check could see this — every triangle present was individually valid; what was wrong was the set. **A structural invariant needs a test of the whole, and rendering the data is a test the numeric checks are not.**
  **NOT in this phase, deliberately not half-done: the coastline.** This is inland land-cover over the baked corridor only, which is why OCEAN never appears in the priority list. `BritishGeographyData._LAND_RLE` is still a whole-hex-quantized mask. Re-deriving the coast from the Natural Earth GB+IRL vectors (7,113 points across GB's 57 sub-polygons, 2,394 across Ireland's 7) needs the land/ocean boundary to become the mesh's outer boundary, which the current chunk-rectangle domain does not model — its own sub-task below, not a flag to flip.
- [ ] **1b: coastline as real vector boundary.** Replace `_LAND_RLE`'s whole-hex quantization for rendering purposes; the mesh domain stops being a chunk rectangle and becomes (chunk ∩ land). **Now the most visible remaining gap, and 1c raised its priority rather than lowering it:** with the whole landmass baked and the sea actually drawn, every coastal chunk still labels its own sea area as `DEFAULT_CLASS` and paints land colour over water. The pure-sea chunk filter (1c) removes the open-ocean case only. - [x] **1c: full GB+Ireland extent, from a Geofabrik `.pbf` (2026-08-18).** Coverage before this: **1,940 of the map's 4,692 land hexes (41.3%)**, and only 80.6% even of the corridor the bake was pointed at — the Overpass fetch bbox came from the projection's calibration points rather than the map's own extent, so 462 corridor land hexes across 46 chunks had **no cached OSM at all**. Everything outside fell back to `HexGridMap`'s flat single-biome tile.
  **Wheel gate passed, unlike last time.** `osmium` (pyosmium) 4.3.1 ships a real `cp314-cp314-win_amd64` wheel — checked with `pip download --only-binary=:all:` rather than `pip index`, which lists PyPI versions and would NOT have caught the `triangle` failure. `pyrosm` also has one; `osmread` does not.
  **New `tools/geo_bake/extract_pbf.py`** streams `great-britain` (2,061 MB) + `ireland-and-northern-ireland` (391 MB) into **the same tile format `fetch_overpass.py` writes** — same `<minlat>_<minlon>_<maxlat>_<maxlon>.json` names, same Overpass `out geom` shape — so the bake cannot tell where a tile came from and the Overpass cache stays intact beside it. Two design points: relation members are emitted as their own single-member relation elements rather than reassembled (`elements_to_features()` already flattens one feature per member ring, so the result is identical without holding a country's relations in memory), and a way is written to **every tile its bbox touches**, mirroring how an Overpass bbox query returns intersecting rather than contained ways. Result: 3.3M ways → 282 tiles, 3.0 GB, 10.1 min, **zero ways skipped for missing node locations**.
  **Validated against the known-good source before trusting it.** Same Manchester chunk baked from each cache, compared by per-class share of chunk AREA (not triangle count, which moves with tessellation): every class within **0.1 percentage point**, triangles within 0.2%. The residue is real OSM edits between the two snapshots.
  **Two problems found and fixed before the long run.** (1) **Tile-cache thrash:** a 0.5-degree tile is ~5,640 wu against a 4,096 wu chunk, so a full-map row spans ~38 tiles and row-major iteration would have reparsed nearly every tile once per row against a cache holding 8 — the same shape as the thrash that once cost 136s of a 156s chunk. Switched to 4x4 blocked iteration and raised capacity to 16; verified purely a locality change, all four test chunks **byte-identical**. (2) **Green sea:** the zero-feature skip only caught chunks with NO features — `chunk 27,6: 1 feats` still painted a full 40km default grid, and 21 of the first 126 chunks written on the first attempt were pure sea. Added `land_chunks()`, parsed from `BritishGeographyData._LAND_RLE` rather than duplicated, testing each hex's EXTENT not just its centre (which catches 17 coastal chunks a centre-only test misses). That also cuts the grid from **1,802 chunks to 289**, since most of a UK+Ireland bounding box is water.
  The bake gained `--cache` and `--full-map` so the extent is no longer hardcoded to the corridor.
  **Result, measured:** 281 chunks written / 8 skipped for genuinely having no geometry, **10,455,221 triangles, 5,269,292 vertices, 89.9 MB**, in 44.9 min. Coverage went **41.3% -> 99.9% of the map's 4,692 land hexes** (4,685), and 100% of the old corridor. The 7 hexes still uncovered are remote offshore islands in 2 chunks ((17,22) west of Ireland, (30,12) in the northern isles) where the OSM extract carries no land-cover polygon at all — a data-availability fact, not a pipeline gap. Worst chunk 53,110 verts against the uint16 limit of 65,536 (**1.23x margin**, down from 1.36x; zero chunks refused). Blocked iteration held tile parses to **377 for 281 chunks** — near-optimal locality, where row-major would have been in the thousands. **`CHUNK_SIZE_WU` is the lever if that vertex margin ever needs widening**; it is the one number that trades file count against per-chunk vertex count.

- [x] **3a: mesh rendering, textured but unblended (2026-08-18).** New `scripts/world/TerrainMeshView.gd`, one node under `WorldRoot`, streaming baked chunks around the camera and drawing them as one `MeshInstance2D` per (biome, soil) pair — a handful of draw calls for ~40,000 triangles where `SubHexGroundView` needs 121 `Sprite2D` nodes for a single hex. Textures are the SAME hand-drawn SVGs `TerrainVisuals` already provides, tiled in WORLD space at `SUB_HEX_GRID_SPAN/11` so density is unchanged from the squares — the shape of the boundaries changed, the art did not.
  **z-order: the whole ground stack moved down one** rather than the mesh moving up. Everything at 0 and above is an entity, so -1 is the only key the mesh can hold without outranking a building; `SubHexGroundView`'s ground went -1 → -2 and `HexGridMap`'s flat Strategic tile -2 → -3. Two ground layers sharing one z_index resolves by tree order, which is the exact flicker-as-hexes-hydrate failure both of those classes' comments already record.
  **`SubHexGroundView` was retired on 2026-08-18, ahead of the 1b/1c coverage this entry expected to wait for** — see the "square ground removed" item below for what that cost and what it did not.
  **Two measured costs.** A chunk build is ~90–170 ms for the densest chunk (68k triangles) and is capped at one per frame, so entering a new dense chunk costs a single ~150 ms hitch; chunks are 40 km wide, so it is rare. 85 ms of an original 145 ms was `SubHexSoilQuery` evaluating its `FastNoiseLite` field per triangle — memoized per rendered texture cell (the granularity the square grid already had), 68,123 triangles collapse to 6,105 distinct cells and the loop drops to 40 ms. `SubHexSoilQuery` gained a public `soil_for_biome_at()` for this: the mesh triangle already states its biome, and routing it back through `soil_fertility_at()` would re-derive that from the raster — and disagree with the vector data wherever the two differ, which is the thing this epic exists to fix.
- [x] **Rivers and water features re-baked from smoothed centrelines (2026-08-18).** "can we look at normalising rivers and water features so they look more natural" (user). A waterway arrives as a LINE and is buffered into a 45m ribbon (4.62 wu), but the ribbon was then simplified at the shared `SIMPLIFY_TOLERANCE_M` of 300m — **30.8 wu, or 6.7x the ribbon's own width**. Measured on one Manchester tile: river area 58,558 -> 120,729 wu2 (**+106%**) and mean width 4.52 -> **9.33 wu**, so rivers drew at double their true width with the width wandering along their length.
  **It was a gameplay bug too, not only an ugly river.** The inflation is not uniform — eroding the result by 1.0 wu breaks it into **36 pieces**, against 10 for the fixed version. Those pinch points are narrower than the 30m sub-cell, which is exactly the barrier leak `WATERWAY_HALF_WIDTH_WU`'s 1.5-sub-cell floor exists to prevent (`HexPathfinder.is_water_crossing_blocked()` samples per sub-cell). The simplify step was quietly undoing the floor.
  **Fix inverts the order:** simplify the CENTRELINE (`WATERWAY_LINE_TOLERANCE_M = 60`), round it with one Chaikin pass (convex — it cannot overshoot the real course), then buffer at `resolution=8`; the merged blob is then simplified at a fraction of its own half-width instead of the shared tolerance. Width comes out 3.95 wu against a true 4.19. `natural=water` maps to the same WATERWAY class, so lakes and reservoirs got the finer boundary as well.
  **Cost: +33%** — 3.53M -> 4.68M triangles, 30.3 -> 40.3 MB, worst chunk 39,737 -> 48,062 verts (margin under the uint16 limit narrowed from 1.65x to 1.36x; the bake refuses rather than wraps, so a breach is loud). **Rivers now render at roughly HALF their previous apparent width, because the previous width was wrong** — 45m in an 8km hex genuinely is a hairline, and they are sub-pixel at the widest tactical zoom. `TYPICAL_RIVER_WIDTH_M` is the single lever if legibility beats fidelity.

- [x] **Fog of war did not cover the vector mesh (fixed 2026-08-18).** User report: "theres a bug where if you zoom into tactical view, the fog of war disappears and you can see the entire UK". PRE-DATED the square-ground removal — it arrived with 3a. Two independent causes, both of which had to go:
  1. **z-order.** `HexCellView`'s fog overlay is its own child and so inherited z_index -3, under the mesh at -1. Mesh moved to -2 and the overlay pinned to an absolute `FogVisuals.TERRAIN_OVERLAY_Z_INDEX = -1` (`z_as_relative = false`, since it has to escape its parent's depth to cover a layer that is not its child).
  2. **The overlay never actually hid anything.** Darkening came from `modulate` on the per-hex node; a world-wide mesh has no per-hex node to tint. State is now stored and replayed in `_ready()`. And verifying at deep zoom proved nothing: the whole viewport sat INSIDE the explored cluster, so an unfogged screen was correct there. **Fog has to be checked at the WIDE end of Tactical, where unexplored hexes are actually on screen.**

### 🧩 Reserved mechanics — data/cost is wired, no consumer system exists yet, no slot in the order above
Each of these has its cost/capacity/upkeep fully in the catalog and is buildable/trainable today; the *effect* the doc describes for it has no code reading it. Flagging them here rather than letting them sit as silent no-ops — none has been scheduled into the implementation order yet, this is a discovery list, not a commitment.

- **5 Tier 4-5 buildings' function-only bonuses have no consuming system.** `BuildingCatalog.gd`'s own per-building doc comments already flag each one: Steam Excavator Depot ("+50% raw node extraction yield across sector" — no sector-wide yield multiplier system), Heavy Coal Washery & Pulverizer ("+100% output of connected smelters/foundries" — no "connected" adjacency system), Mechanized Maintenance Depot ("continuous auto-repair in ZoC" — no consumer reads this), Macadamized Transport Hub ("+50% transfer throughput and speed" — no logistics-throughput multiplier system), Automated Freight Marshalling Yard ("rail +500%, zero transfer delay" — no rail-speed-bonus system). All 5 were scoped this way deliberately in the Building tree rework (PR #57) rather than inventing 5 new subsystems in one pass — each needs its own design pass (sector/adjacency definitions, a repair-tick mechanic, a logistics-throughput model, rail vs. road/canal speed handling) before it can do anything beyond cost Pop/Energy/Coal for flavor.
- **Vehicle Fuel Reserve is recorded but inert.** `design_doc.md` §4's `Fuel Reserve: 50 Coal` line (identical across all 6 Tier 4-5 vehicles) lives in `UnitDefinition.fuel_reserve` (added in the Unit tree rework, PR #58) but nothing reads it — no refuel/depletion mechanic exists. Today a vehicle's only real Coal cost is its recurring `daily_upkeep[COAL]` drain; the "fixed tank, refuel logistics" half of the doc's vehicle-fuel concept isn't built.
- **Per-building unit-tier training isn't enforced.** `design_doc.md` §4 assigns each unit tier to one specific building (T0 Town Hall, T1 Garrison, T2 Armory & Barracks, T3 High Command & Cavalry Depot, T4 Traction Works & Workshop, T5 Ordnance & Armament Complex), and `BuildingCatalog.gd` sets `can_train_units = true` on exactly those 6 — but `UnitManager.train_unit()` takes no building-specific filter, so any of the 6 can train any tier the colony has researched (`TechManager.is_unit_tier_unlocked()` is a global gate, not a per-building one). A Town Hall can train a Tier 5 unit today, once Tier 5 tech is researched. `BuildMenuView._trainable_unit_roles()`'s own doc comment already names this as current, accurate behavior, not a bug — worth a real design pass (does the doc's assignment matter for gameplay, or is "any training building trains any researched tier" the intended simplification?) rather than assuming which way it should go.
- **`BuildingDefinition.storage_bonus` has a mechanism and a consumer but nothing populates it.** `BuildingManager.place_building()` already applies it and `ResourceManager.add_storage_cap()` already tracks it — but no building in the current 42-entry catalog sets it (the one building that used to, the pre-rework Grain Silo, was cut in the Building tree rework for having no `design_doc.md` equivalent). Different from the other three above: `design_doc.md` never describes a storage-cap-boosting building at all, so this isn't a doc mechanic waiting on a scheduling slot — it's pre-doc infrastructure with nothing left to drive it. Worth a decision (revive as a real doc-adjacent building, or strip the dead field/code path) rather than leaving it silently orphaned.

## 🔩 Other Open Items (not covered by design_doc.md)
- [ ] **Resource-tick pacing/balancing** (flagged by the user 2026-08-11, needs more thought before touching): production ticks once per in-game day; default game speed is 5x. Measured directly on the file: pixels at `x%4` in {0,1,2} are identical to their right neighbour 100% of the time. That upsample, not source-tile quantisation, is what produced the rectangular terracing `ReliefImageBuilder._smoothed()` blurs away (its comment has been corrected). New: `tools/geo_bake/terrarium_mosaic.py` (bulk, **bilinear**, numpy-vectorised Terrarium sampling at any zoom, with parallel prefetch -- the per-point `fetch_terrarium.terrarium_elevation_at()` would have needed ~430M calls) and `tools/geo_bake/bake_fine_relief.py`, which writes one greyscale hillshade PNG per hex to `assets/terrain_data/relief/<q>_<r>.png` at 333x333 = **30 m/px, ~333x333 values per hex against ~3x3**, from Terrarium z12 (~22.5 m/px, finer than the output grid so nothing is upsampled). **Stores SHADE, not metres**, for two reasons: one byte per pixel instead of three, and -- decisive -- a hillshade needs the elevation GRADIENT, so computing it per tile at runtime would have to invent values past each tile's edge and every tile boundary would show a discontinuity; baking it from one continuous field with a 2 px margin makes adjacent tiles agree exactly. **Tile origins are snapped to a global sample lattice** (`tile_origin_world()` / `ReliefTileView.tile_origin()`, which must stay in step): tiles span 1024 wu but hex centres are closer than that, so tiles overlap, and unsnapped origins would make two tiles sample the same ground at different sub-pixel offsets and disagree by a shade step -- a faint hex lattice over the whole map. Verified: adjacent tiles differ by an integral 288 px offset and are **bit-for-bit identical across their 45x333 overlap**. Rendering is a two-layer LOD, same shape `TerrainMeshView` has against `HexGridMap`'s flat tile: `ElevationReliefView` keeps the whole-corridor coarse texture (Strategic), new `ReliefTileView` streams the 30 m tiles for on-screen hexes in Tactical only, capped at `MAX_TILES_IN_VIEW`. Shade -> light/shadow conversion is a **GPU shader** (`assets/shaders/relief_hillshade.gdshader`, neutral 128 becomes fully transparent so flat ground is untouched and only slopes modulate the art) rather than per-pixel RGBA rebuilding on load, which measured ~0.25 s/tile in GDScript. **Consequence stated plainly: sub-hex elevation in METRES is still not stored anywhere** -- nothing needs it (mountain impassability is per macro hex on purpose), and the z12 tiles stay in `tools/geo_bake/cache/` (gitignored) so adding a metres channel later is a re-bake, not a re-fetch. **Two defects found only by RENDERING the result, both fixed:** (1) the shading constant `_SLOPE_METRES_AT_FULL` was 12.0 m per 30 m pixel — a 40% grade almost nothing in the corridor reaches — so every real hillside landed in the bottom few percent of the range and the map still read flat; chosen properly at **3.5** (~12% grade) via `tools/geo_bake/compare_relief_slope.py`, which bakes one hex at several values side by side (measured byte spread: std 25.0 at 12.0, 46.9 at 5.0, **55.2 at 3.5**, 64.4 at 2.0 where it saturates). (2) tiles drawn as full squares produced a **visible rectangular grid** across the map: they overlap, and alpha **composites twice** in the doubly-covered region even though the lattice snap makes the two tiles' values bit-for-bit identical there — verifying values match is not sufficient for an alpha overlay, each pixel must be drawn ONCE. Fixed by drawing each tile as a `Polygon2D` clipped to its hexagon (hexagons tile the plane exactly) rather than a full-square `Sprite2D`; the artefact was invisible until the shading was strong enough to see it. Final cost: **3,876 tiles, 157 MB, 11 min** of CPU for a re-bake (the ~14,000 z12 source tiles are cached); the first run's fetch was 100% network-latency-bound and parallel prefetch (16 workers) cut it from an extrapolated ~4 hours to ~17 min. Verified in-engine over real terrain art via `scripts/test/preview_relief_ingame.gd` — a light scene (TerrainMeshView + ReliefTileView + camera only, deliberately not `Main.tscn`) that screenshots to `user://`; it must run WITHOUT `--headless` to rasterise.

### ⚔️ Threat escalation — the structural gap the playtest surfaced (2026-08-17)
- [ ] **Nothing escalates over time; the colony was unthreatened for 149 consecutive days** (observed, needs a user design decision before any code): across days 1–149 at 1000x the session saw no enemy sprite, alert, counter, bearing or timer, and the HUD carries no wave number, no ETA and no enemy count. The first and only combat text arrived on day 150 (`Smallholding Farm destroyed at (80, 116)!`) — a building the player never saw, at coordinates the UI gives no way to navigate to. Per the standing *They Are Billions* directive above, TAB's actual structure was researched rather than invented: **10 scheduled swarms** at a fixed cadence scaled to map length, each announced **8 game-hours ahead with a compass bearing**, the final swarm given **24 hours'** notice and landing at **~90% of map length** (not the last day, so it must actually be fought), plus unannounced runner trickle from **day 20** so the perimeter is never safe to strip ([Swarms wiki](https://they-are-billions.fandom.com/wiki/Swarms), [Swarm days/size by difficulty](https://steamcommunity.com/app/644930/discussions/0/1849197902656427682/)). Concrete proposal for a 100-day map, as a starting point rather than a spec: swarms on days **12, 22, 31, 39, 47, 55, 63, 72, 81, 90 (final)**, sized **40 / 58 / 84 / 122 / 177 / 257 / 372 / 540 / 783** from one edge and **4,000 from all edges** on day 90; 2–6 infected/day from a random edge from day 20; and a permanent HUD readout (`Swarm 4/10 — 2d 6h — North-East`) turning red at T-8h, T-24h for the final. **This changes what the game fundamentally is — a fixed-length siege campaign rather than an open-ended builder — so it needs an explicit user decision on map length and whether the campaign is fixed-length at all before anything is written.** Today's only day-driven spawn hook (`HordeManager._on_ambient_spawn_day`) takes the day number as an unused `_day_number` parameter, i.e. day 80 is exactly as dangerous as day 3.
