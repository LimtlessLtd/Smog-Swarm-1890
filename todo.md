## 🔧 Technical Summary
Godot 4.x/GDScript RTS on a continuous axial hex map of real UK+Ireland geography (Natural Earth coastline, ~25 sq mi/hex, ~4,700 land hexes). Terrain inside the playable England corridor (Manchester/Midlands/London) is real data baked from OpenStreetMap land-cover + SRTM elevation (`tools/geo_bake/`), rendered at both a coarse whole-corridor resolution and per-hex fine tiles near the camera. A two-tier camera (Strategic hex-icon / Tactical true-scale, `HexCoord.HEX_SIZE` = 512 world units/hex) drives everything from country-scale strategy to individual soldiers. **Phases 1 → 2.12, 4, 5 (core), 6, and 6.3 are built and playable end to end** — full economy, building tree, tech tree, 18-unit roster with combat/morale/veterancy, horde AI, walls/ZoC/supply lines, Fog of War, Day/Night, save/load, and a complete code-drawn HUD. **Not built:** the sewer/underground layer (Phase 3), late-game reclamation and the campaign (Phase 5.11, Phase 7). ## 📖 The Setting
January 1890. Something came down over the Pennines in the night, and within a week the dead of Manchester were walking. The city sealed itself hard — gaslit checkpoints, a militia raised from constables and navvies, whatever the Industrial Revolution could throw up in a fortnight — and held. Beyond the wire, the rest of Britain went dark: no telegraph, no trains, no light after sundown, just the slow drift of the hungry across a country that used to be the workshop of the world. You inherit that quarantine: one lit town in an island of soot and silence, armed with nothing but cast iron, coal smoke, and Victorian grit — no borrowed future technology, just the Industrial Revolution pushed as far as it will go. South of you, Queen Victoria and the Imperial Cabinet are sealed in a bunker under the Tower of London, and nobody above ground knows if they're still alive. Scattered through the wreckage are stranger traces still — wrecked observatories, craters that don't match any war, rumours of things that didn't fall from the sky by accident. Your job isn't rescue. It's to hold the light, push roads and rail south hex by hex, and find out — expedition by expedition — whether Manchester's quarantine was the last sane decision the old world made, or the first sign that whatever did this was already here.

## 🏛️ Project Architecture & Design Overview
- **Core Loop:** Continuous persistent expansion across Victorian Britain hex sectors. Build defenses, automate patrols, manage daily resource upkeeps (Food/Energy/Gunpowder), and protect supply lines while working in the background.
- **Aesthetic Direction:** Grounded late-18th/19th-century Industrial Revolution (brick, cast iron, coal smoke, authentic Victorian architecture)—explicitly **no** retro-futuristic steampunk tropes, including top-tier "mechanized" units (heavy steam engineering, never a humanoid battle-mech).
- **Dual Perspective Engine:** Camera toggle between Top-Down Orthographic and 2.5D Isometric.
- **Multi-Layer Macro-Hex System:** Each hex = a ~25 sq mi macro-region. Cities scale across multiple macro-tiles — Manchester spans 4 hexes, Greater London spans 12. Internal district partitioning and subterranean layers (Sewers/Underground) planned.
- **Time Controls & Day/Night Cycle:** Global `TickManager` supporting `0x`/`1x`/`2x`/`3x`/`5x` speeds. 40-minute real-time day/night cycle at 1x.
- **Accessibility:** Every color-coded piece of state (biome, ZoC, fog-of-war, building category, alerts) is paired with a distinct shape/icon, never color alone.
- **World State Framing:** Outside player-held ground, 1890 Britain is dark and roamed by hordes with no fixed home. Player settlements are the only lit, defended points on the map. Arc runs from defensive "hold the light" early-game toward reclaiming and re-lighting the country late-game (Phase 5.11, Phase 7.4/7.5).

## 🧩 Known Architecture Constraint: Hex-Grid Over-Coupling
> One hex is ~25 sq. miles — most fields below are still a single scalar value for that entire area, which doesn't match real terrain. Biomes/elevation and wall geometry are already real sub-hex resolution (closed 2026-08-10 and earlier). Still hex-locked today:
- **Supply lines** (`SupplyLineSegment.hex_a`/`hex_b`) — whole-edge only, the same shape walls used to have before their rework.
- **Zone of Control, Noise, Fog of War** (`LogisticsNetwork`, `NoiseManager`, `FogOfWarManager`) — flat per-hex `Dictionary` state, no gradient across the hex's own area.
- **Districts** (`District.gd`/`DistrictPartitioner.gd`) — fixed categorical template per hex (always exactly Urban Center + Industrial Estate + Uncleared Wilderness for a settlement hex), no real position/shape/geography.

Revisit if/when a dedicated sub-hex spatial partition gets designed for these — Districts' `Array[District]` per hex is the closest existing seam to extend, though terrain's own fix used a sampled raster instead of a partition since it's read-heavy/write-never data, not the same shape as ZoC/Districts' write-heavy contested-flag-flips-at-runtime need.

**Also disclosed:** the full map span (~214,600×136,700 world units) is past the ~100,000-unit range where single-precision float jitter typically starts (`HexCoord.HEX_SIZE`'s doc comment) — no jitter observed live so far; a floating-origin camera rebase is the real fix if it ever surfaces.

### 🗺️ Sub-Hex Mechanical Layer (new epic, 2026-08-16 — supersedes the old Biomes item above)
User directive: hex-tiles are for visibility/aesthetics ONLY — each displays whichever biome is most common within it (majority-voted from many sub-hex samples, exactly as `HexCell.biome_type` already works today), but every MECHANIC (movement, resource extraction, building placement, vision/LoS, Zone of Control, horde AI) moves onto a real, persistent, **30m-resolution sub-hex grid** underneath. Aim: a much larger-scale "They Are Billions"-style game across Britain and the British Isles, 1890s, with genuine zoomed-in play (individual units/people/buildings), while the map itself stays enormous (playable corridor alone is 3,876 macro-hexes, ~5km each).

Full reasoning, the confirmed architecture, and the two "picked the wrong default, corrected after user pushback" lessons from how this got decided all live in the **`sub-hex-mechanical-layer-epic`** Claude memory file — read that before touching any of these phases, don't re-derive it from scratch. Short version of the confirmed architecture (none of this should be re-litigated without new information):
- Rendering and mechanics are explicitly **decoupled** — fine visual zoom doesn't require the mechanics grid to match rendering resolution (units/buildings already have continuous, non-grid-snapped positions; `SubHexGroundView` already renders fine terrain detail).
- 30m mechanics tiles, confirmed deliberately after being shown the naive-dense-storage cost (~430M cells for the corridor) and choosing to commit anyway once shown the architecture that makes it tractable — **not** a compromise/scaled-down number.
- **Sparse, not dense, storage** — on-demand/cached raster lookups for the 99%+ of cells nothing ever mutates (extends `RealTerrainSampler`'s existing pattern); only real gameplay mutations get persistent state, matching `ReclamationManager._drained_hexes`'s existing sparse-`Dictionary` pattern.
- **Hierarchical (HPA*-style) pathfinding** for long-range routing — an abstract per-macro-hex-cluster graph for the long search, refined to real 30m steps only within traversed clusters. Chosen because the user explicitly wants full sub-hex resolution even for long-range routing, and a flat full-resolution search is untenable given `HordeManager`'s own existing "hundreds-to-thousands of hordes" performance comments at today's far coarser resolution.
- **Regional flow-field movement for hordes** specifically (not per-horde A*) — one shared cost-field per active goal/region.
- Real 30m fidelity needs the bake pipeline rebaked at ~12x more pixels per tile than today's ~104m/pixel fine tiles — its own deferred sub-phase, not bundled into the data-model work.

**Phases** (each its own reviewed PR, same one-item-at-a-time discipline as everything else in this file):
- [x] **1a: sparse sub-hex terrain query layer (2026-08-16).** New `HexCoord.gd` sub-hex coordinate math (`SUB_HEX_CELL_SIZE_METERS = 30.0`, `world_to_sub_hex()`/`sub_hex_to_world()`/`sub_hex_index_within()`) and new `SubHexTerrainQuery.gd` (cached, on-demand, reuses `RealTerrainSampler.sample_at_hex()` — a new public method extracted from `sample_grid()`'s own fine-tile-preferring logic — against today's existing bake data, no bake changes yet). `SubHexGroundView` repointed to it as the correctness proof. **Verified, not assumed, that this isn't byte-identical to the old direct-sampling path** (documented in `SubHexGroundView._sample_render_grid()`'s own comment): `SubHexTerrainQuery` snaps every query to its 30m cell's center (correct for a real mechanical grid — nearby positions in one cell must agree), which for a hex WITH a baked fine tile only ever recovers real data at edge samples that used to return empty (strict improvement), but for the ~99% of the corridor WITHOUT one yet, can occasionally cross into a neighboring coarse pixel near a boundary and show an adjacent biome classification (~5% of one test hex's render-grid samples) — a real, small, edge-of-hex-scale rendering difference, not a crash, and moot once Phase 1b rebakes real 30m data. No mechanic (movement/extraction/vision/ZoC/horde AI) reads any of this yet — all still on `HexCell.biome_type` exactly as before.
- [x] **1b: rebake fine-tile pipeline at real 30m-equivalent resolution, full 3,876-hex playable corridor (2026-08-16).** `tools/geo_bake/bake_fine_tiles.py` gained a `--corridor` mode (reuses `bake_landcover.py`'s `CORRIDOR_Q`/`CORRIDOR_R` and, deliberately, its `lonlat_bbox_from_calibration_points()` bbox verbatim rather than deriving one from the hex set — confirmed this makes `fetch_bbox_tiled()`'s tile cache keys land exactly on the 113 tiles/~1.86M OSM elements the coarse whole-corridor bake had already cached, so the corridor-wide fine bake fetched **zero new Overpass tiles**, purely local CPU rasterization) and `FINE_TILE_PIXELS` raised 96→333 (matches `HexCoord.SUB_HEX_GRID_N` from 1a exactly — one baked pixel per 30m mechanical sub-cell, not an approximation). Added a spatial feature index (`_FeatureIndex`, bucketed by projected world-space bbox) — the old per-hex `_feature_might_touch()` linear-scanned the entire fetched feature list per hex, intractable at 3,876 hexes against ~1.6M ring features; bucketing made each hex's query touch only nearby buckets. Also added per-hex resumability (skips existing tiles unless `--force`). Measured: dense-urban worst case (100-hex Manchester cluster) rasterized at ~2s/hex after one-time ~90-150s setup (JSON load + index build); full corridor run completed in 1,430s (~24min), wrote 3,876 tiles (26MB total), no crashes, no zero-byte files. Verified via `--headless scenes/main/Main.tscn --quit` (exit 0, no errors — only the pre-existing "not export-safe" `Image.load()` warning, now firing more often since coverage is corridor-wide instead of a 37-hex demo). `RealTerrainSampler.WORLD_UNITS_PER_PIXEL_FINE` updated to `1024.0/333.0` to match. Every corridor hex now has genuine (not aliased) 30m fidelity where a fine tile exists — `SubHexTerrainQuery`'s Phase 1a-documented cell-center-snap aliasing only still applies to hexes outside the corridor (Wales/Scotland/Ireland, hard-gated, unreached).
- [x] **1c: hierarchical (HPA*) pathfinding graph structure (2026-08-16).** The abstract per-cluster graph itself turned out to already exist — `HexGridMap`'s macro-hex adjacency plus `HexPathfinder`'s A* over it IS the top-level "one node per cluster" graph the epic's own roadmap describes. What Phase 1c actually added is the layer underneath: new `scripts/world/data/SubHexPortal.gd` (a portal — one passable 30m-resolution crossing point between two adjacent macro hexes) and new `scripts/world/SubHexPortalGraph.gd` (`find_portals(hex_grid_map, hex_a, hex_b)`, lazy + cached per unordered hex pair — same sparse/on-demand convention `SubHexTerrainQuery` established, deliberately NOT precomputed for the whole 3,876-hex corridor at once, which would force every fine-tile PNG decoded and resident in memory simultaneously, ~1.3GB, for a graph most playthroughs never fully traverse). Portal-finding geometry: a regular hexagon's edge length equals its own circumradius (`HexCoord.HEX_SIZE`), so the boundary shared by two adjacent hex centers is just the segment perpendicular to the line joining them, centred at its midpoint, spanning `HEX_SIZE` — true for any adjacent pair, no per-direction corner lookup table needed. Samples that shared edge at `SUB_HEX_CELL_SIZE_WORLD_UNITS` intervals, checks passability on both sides (reapplies `HexCell.is_passable()`'s exact MARSH/PEAT_BOG/OCEAN rule at sub-hex granularity via `SubHexTerrainQuery` where real baked data exists; falls back to the macro hex's own `is_passable()` outside the baked corridor, the same "empty result -> fall back to flat default" contract `HexMapGenerator` already established), and collapses each contiguous passable run into one portal at its midpoint (classic HPA* "one entrance = one node" simplification). Verified via a temporary debug hook in `HexGridMap.generate_map()` (removed before commit) exercising 4 adjacent-hex pairs during a real `--headless scenes/main/Main.tscn --quit` boot: deep-corridor pairs each resolved exactly 1 portal with geometrically sane, direction-appropriate sub-index mirroring (e.g. a `(1,0)`-direction pair kept the same local y-index on both sides while x flipped near-max<->near-min, exactly as expected for that edge orientation); a pair straddling the corridor boundary correctly exercised the macro-hex fallback path with no crash. No consumer wired in yet — Phase 2 (movement) is what will call this to refine a macro-hex route into a real sub-hex-accurate path; this phase only builds and proves the portal structure, same "infra with no consumer yet" restraint Phase 1a's `SubHexTerrainQuery` took.
- [x] **2a: hierarchical long-range routing through real sub-hex portals (2026-08-16).** `SubHexPortalGraph.gd` gained `portal_offset_for_step(hex_grid_map, from_hex, to_hex)` — the local offset (relative to `to_hex`'s own center) a mover should aim for when crossing that edge, resolved from `find_portals()`'s real 30m crossing points (Phase 1c) rather than always the hex's plain geometric center. When more than one portal exists along an edge, picks whichever sits closest to the edge midpoint (the center-to-center line's own closest point to the edge, since that line is perpendicular to the edge by construction — Phase 1c's own geometry) — the least-detour crossing. Falls back to `Vector2.ZERO` (the pre-2a plain-center behavior) when `find_portals()` returns none for that edge (HexPathfinder's macro graph only sees per-hex passability, not per-edge boundary detail, so a traversable hex pair can still have a fully-impassable shared boundary). `UnitOrderController._advance_toward()` and `HordeManager._advance_horde()` — which already shared the identical hex-crossing shape — both call this per leg and feed the result into `MovementStepper.advance_toward_hex()`'s existing `target_local_offset` param (previously only ever used for a player order's final clicked point), so a multi-hex route now walks a straight chord from one edge's real crossing point to the next instead of always detouring through every intermediate hex's dead center. Both managers' live wall-blocking re-check (`WallManager.get_blocking_segment()`) was updated to test against this same portal-adjusted endpoint rather than the plain hex center, so the check tests the exact line the mover is about to walk. Verified via a temporary debug hook in `HexGridMap.generate_map()` (removed before commit) calling `portal_offset_for_step()` for 4 real adjacent-hex pairs during a `--headless scenes/main/Main.tscn --quit` boot: every offset's magnitude landed at ~443.4 world units, matching the expected center-to-boundary-midpoint distance (`HEX_SIZE * sqrt(3)/2`) exactly, and two pairs sharing the same relative direction but at very different map locations ((110,130)->(111,129) and (80,90)->(81,89), both the (+1,-1) neighbor direction) produced near-identical offsets — confirms the geometry is direction-derived, not coordinate-derived. ### 🧩 Reserved mechanics — data/cost is wired, no consumer system exists yet, no slot in the order above
Each of these has its cost/capacity/upkeep fully in the catalog and is buildable/trainable today; the *effect* the doc describes for it has no code reading it. Flagging them here rather than letting them sit as silent no-ops — none has been scheduled into the implementation order yet, this is a discovery list, not a commitment.

- **5 Tier 4-5 buildings' function-only bonuses have no consuming system.** `BuildingCatalog.gd`'s own per-building doc comments already flag each one: Steam Excavator Depot ("+50% raw node extraction yield across sector" — no sector-wide yield multiplier system), Heavy Coal Washery & Pulverizer ("+100% output of connected smelters/foundries" — no "connected" adjacency system), Mechanized Maintenance Depot ("continuous auto-repair in ZoC" — no consumer reads this), Macadamized Transport Hub ("+50% transfer throughput and speed" — no logistics-throughput multiplier system), Automated Freight Marshalling Yard ("rail +500%, zero transfer delay" — no rail-speed-bonus system). All 5 were scoped this way deliberately in the Building tree rework (PR #57) rather than inventing 5 new subsystems in one pass — each needs its own design pass (sector/adjacency definitions, a repair-tick mechanic, a logistics-throughput model, rail vs. road/canal speed handling) before it can do anything beyond cost Pop/Energy/Coal for flavor.
- **Vehicle Fuel Reserve is recorded but inert.** `design_doc.md` §4's `Fuel Reserve: 50 Coal` line (identical across all 6 Tier 4-5 vehicles) lives in `UnitDefinition.fuel_reserve` (added in the Unit tree rework, PR #58) but nothing reads it — no refuel/depletion mechanic exists. Today a vehicle's only real Coal cost is its recurring `daily_upkeep[COAL]` drain; the "fixed tank, refuel logistics" half of the doc's vehicle-fuel concept isn't built.
- **Per-building unit-tier training isn't enforced.** `design_doc.md` §4 assigns each unit tier to one specific building (T0 Town Hall, T1 Garrison, T2 Armory & Barracks, T3 High Command & Cavalry Depot, T4 Traction Works & Workshop, T5 Ordnance & Armament Complex), and `BuildingCatalog.gd` sets `can_train_units = true` on exactly those 6 — but `UnitManager.train_unit()` takes no building-specific filter, so any of the 6 can train any tier the colony has researched (`TechManager.is_unit_tier_unlocked()` is a global gate, not a per-building one). A Town Hall can train a Tier 5 unit today, once Tier 5 tech is researched. `BuildMenuView._trainable_unit_roles()`'s own doc comment already names this as current, accurate behavior, not a bug — worth a real design pass (does the doc's assignment matter for gameplay, or is "any training building trains any researched tier" the intended simplification?) rather than assuming which way it should go.
- **`BuildingDefinition.storage_bonus` has a mechanism and a consumer but nothing populates it.** `BuildingManager.place_building()` already applies it and `ResourceManager.add_storage_cap()` already tracks it — but no building in the current 42-entry catalog sets it (the one building that used to, the pre-rework Grain Silo, was cut in the Building tree rework for having no `design_doc.md` equivalent). Different from the other three above: `design_doc.md` never describes a storage-cap-boosting building at all, so this isn't a doc mechanic waiting on a scheduling slot — it's pre-doc infrastructure with nothing left to drive it. Worth a decision (revive as a real doc-adjacent building, or strip the dead field/code path) rather than leaving it silently orphaned.

## 🔩 Other Open Items (not covered by design_doc.md)
- [ ] **Resource-tick pacing/balancing** (flagged by the user 2026-08-11, needs more thought before touching): production ticks once per in-game day; default game speed is 5x. Should this move to multiple ticks/day (e.g. "4 might reduce to 2 ticks a day and 2 ticks a night")? Any change has to solve the same catch-up-safety problem the existing flat day/night average multiplier was built to sidestep — `TickManager.day_completed` can fire multiple times in one frame during a large-delta catch-up burst at high speed, so a naive per-phase accumulator would double/under-count.
- [ ] **Ambient ruins from real settlement data** (user request, 2026-08-11, scoped not built): pre-existing rubble scattered across unowned/uncontrolled land based on where real UK settlements actually exist, distinct from the player-built-then-destroyed ruin mechanic. Needs `tools/geo_bake/fetch_overpass.py`'s query extended with a `building=*` tag (currently only pulls `landuse`/`natural`/`waterway`), a new settlement-footprint raster or density channel, and a consumer (likely `LocalDetailGenerator`) that places ruin props where real OSM data says a building used to stand and the hex isn't currently player-controlled. No mechanics decided for what a ruin actually does beyond visual dressing (loot? salvage? horde spawn bias?) — deferred until a follow-up decides.
- [ ] **Gate pass-through behavior:** gates currently block identically to a plain wall until breached — no special ally-pass-through mechanic yet.
- [ ] **Famine-severity input to Morale:** a fifth input (units undersupplied but not dying) would need `UnitMorale` to take a live `BuildingManager` reference it's currently deliberately free of — reasonable extension, not wired.
- [ ] **Seamless biome blending across hex boundaries** (user request, 2026-08-16): the user wants biome transitions to read as gradual gradients everywhere, not hard edges anywhere. Two distinct gaps, both purely visual/rendering — no mechanical change:
  - **Strategic zoom (`HexCellView`):** each hex renders ONE flat texture from its own majority-vote `biome_type` (`TerrainVisuals.terrain_texture()`) — a hard polygon edge wherever two adjacent hexes' majority biomes differ. No blending exists at this zoom level at all.
  - **Tactical zoom (`SubHexGroundView`):** real cross-biome blending already exists WITHIN one hex (`_compute_blend()` + `terrain_blend.gdshader`, `_MAX_BLEND_WEIGHT = 0.6`) — but each hex's sub-cell grid is explicitly clipped to its own footprint (`_GRID_SPAN`'s own doc comment: "a hex-shaped clip mask... keeps it from overhanging into neighboring hexes"), so the blend never crosses a hex-to-hex boundary. Two independently-rendered hexes sitting side by side still meet at a hard seam even though each one is smooth internally.
  Now that Phase 1b (Sub-Hex Mechanical Layer) has baked real 30m fine-tile data for the entire 3,876-hex corridor, a fix could sample a few sub-cells' worth past each hex's own edge (into the neighboring hex's already-baked fine tile) so `_compute_blend()` has real neighbor data to blend toward right at the seam, instead of stopping at the hex boundary. Strategic zoom's flat-texture hard edges are a separate, bigger question (per-hex single-texture rendering doesn't have blend geometry to work with at all) — worth its own design pass rather than assuming the Tactical-zoom fix generalizes.

### 🕹️ Playtest harness follow-ups (`scripts/test/AgentHarness.gd`)
- [ ] **Game time runs unattended while the playtester thinks** (user-observed, 2026-08-17): the visible "idling" during the session is the observe→decide→act loop — the game sits at whatever speed was last set while the agent reasons about the previous screenshot, which at 1000x means ~12 in-game days can elapse per 30s of thinking, unobserved and unrecorded. Makes sessions non-deterministic and is the likely explanation for the two apparent clock stalls the session logged but declined to file as findings. Fix is a `step_days(n)` command that pauses, advances exactly n days, and pauses again, plus auto-pausing whenever the harness is idle waiting for a command — so game time only moves when the playtester asks for it.
- [x] **Key events could not be held down** (fixed 2026-08-17): `_cmd_key` pressed and released across consecutive frames, so `CameraController._handle_pan_input()` — which polls `Input.is_action_pressed()` per `_process` frame and integrates against delta — panned by one frame's worth, i.e. invisibly. This produced a false finding ("there is no camera control") in the first session. Added a `hold` seconds parameter; measured after the fix: 0.6s with no key changed **0 pixels**, 0.6s holding `D` changed **72,237**. Any future finding about camera, edge-pan or held-input behavior from before this fix should be re-tested rather than trusted.

### ⚔️ Threat escalation — the structural gap the playtest surfaced (2026-08-17)
- [ ] **Nothing escalates over time; the colony was unthreatened for 149 consecutive days** (observed, needs a user design decision before any code): across days 1–149 at 1000x the session saw no enemy sprite, alert, counter, bearing or timer, and the HUD carries no wave number, no ETA and no enemy count. The first and only combat text arrived on day 150 (`Smallholding Farm destroyed at (80, 116)!`) — a building the player never saw, at coordinates the UI gives no way to navigate to. Per the standing *They Are Billions* directive above, TAB's actual structure was researched rather than invented: **10 scheduled swarms** at a fixed cadence scaled to map length, each announced **8 game-hours ahead with a compass bearing**, the final swarm given **24 hours'** notice and landing at **~90% of map length** (not the last day, so it must actually be fought), plus unannounced runner trickle from **day 20** so the perimeter is never safe to strip ([Swarms wiki](https://they-are-billions.fandom.com/wiki/Swarms), [Swarm days/size by difficulty](https://steamcommunity.com/app/644930/discussions/0/1849197902656427682/)). Concrete proposal for a 100-day map, as a starting point rather than a spec: swarms on days **12, 22, 31, 39, 47, 55, 63, 72, 81, 90 (final)**, sized **40 / 58 / 84 / 122 / 177 / 257 / 372 / 540 / 783** from one edge and **4,000 from all edges** on day 90; 2–6 infected/day from a random edge from day 20; and a permanent HUD readout (`Swarm 4/10 — 2d 6h — North-East`) turning red at T-8h, T-24h for the final. **This changes what the game fundamentally is — a fixed-length siege campaign rather than an open-ended builder — so it needs an explicit user decision on map length and whether the campaign is fixed-length at all before anything is written.** Today's only day-driven spawn hook (`HordeManager._on_ambient_spawn_day`) takes the day number as an unused `_day_number` parameter, i.e. day 80 is exactly as dangerous as day 3.
