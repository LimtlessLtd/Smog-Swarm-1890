## 🔧 Technical Summary
Godot 4.x/GDScript RTS built on a continuous axial hex map of real UK+Ireland geography (Natural Earth's public-domain coastline data, recalibrated to a genuine ~25 sq mi/hex scale — 154×179 bounds, ~4,700 land hexes of ~27,566 total). Terrain *inside* that coastline is now real data too, for the playable England corridor (Manchester/Midlands/London + margin): biome/elevation baked from OpenStreetMap land-cover + AWS/Mapzen SRTM-derived elevation (`tools/geo_bake/`), with actual sub-hex variation rendered at Tactical zoom. A two-tier camera (Strategic hex-icon view / Tactical true-scale in-hex view, `HexCoord.HEX_SIZE` = 512 world units per hex) with four LOD bands drives everything from country-scale strategy down to individual soldiers and props. Full economy (8 resource types, 21 building types across Housing/Industry/Agriculture/Defense, a 6-node tech tree), an 18-unit roster (6 tiers × 3 roles) with real combat, morale/veterancy, and per-Special-unit mechanical identities, wandering zombie hordes with noise/light-attraction AI and wall-siege behavior, a full wall/gate/ZoC/supply-line layer, Fog of War, Day/Night cycles, save/load, and a complete code-drawn HUD (build menu, unit orders, minimap, tech tree, alerts). **Phases 1 → 2.12, 4, 5 (core), 6, and 6.3 (including all AI-generated art, see correction below) are built and playable end to end.** Not built: the sewer/underground layer (Phase 3), late-game reclamation and the entire narrative campaign (Phase 5.11, Phase 7). A real, documented architecture debt exists around hex-grid granularity — see that dedicated section below before starting any biome/wall/logistics work.

## 📖 The Setting
January 1890. Something came down over the Pennines in the night, and within a week the dead of Manchester were walking. The city sealed itself hard — gaslit checkpoints, a militia raised from constables and navvies, whatever the Industrial Revolution could throw up in a fortnight — and held. Beyond the wire, the rest of Britain went dark: no telegraph, no trains, no light after sundown, just the slow drift of the hungry across a country that used to be the workshop of the world. You inherit that quarantine: one lit town in an island of soot and silence, armed with nothing but cast iron, coal smoke, and Victorian grit — no borrowed future technology, just the Industrial Revolution pushed as far as it will go. South of you, Queen Victoria and the Imperial Cabinet are sealed in a bunker under the Tower of London, and nobody above ground knows if they're still alive. Scattered through the wreckage are stranger traces still — wrecked observatories, craters that don't match any war, rumours of things that didn't fall from the sky by accident. Your job isn't rescue. It's to hold the light, push roads and rail south hex by hex, and find out — expedition by expedition — whether Manchester's quarantine was the last sane decision the old world made, or the first sign that whatever did this was already here.

## 🏛️ Project Architecture & Design Overview

- **Core Loop:** Continuous persistent expansion across Victorian Britain hex sectors. Build defenses, automate patrols, manage daily resource upkeeps (Food/Energy/Gunpowder), and protect supply lines while working in the background.
- **Aesthetic Direction:** Grounded late-18th/19th-century Industrial Revolution (brick, cast iron, coal smoke, authentic Victorian architecture)—explicitly **no** retro-futuristic steampunk tropes. Applies even to the top-tier "mechanized" units in Phase 5.4 — heavy steam engineering (armoured traction engines, rail guns, breaching cranes), never a humanoid battle-mech.
- **Dual Perspective Engine:** Dynamic camera toggle between **Top-Down Orthographic** (tactical layout) and **2.5D Isometric** (cinematic viewing).
- **Multi-Layer Macro-Hex System:** Continuous tilemap hiding an underlying axial hex grid `(q, r)`. Each hex represents a 5x5 mile macro-region (~25 sq miles). Cities scale dynamically across multiple macro-tiles—Manchester spans 4 hexes (~100 sq miles), while Greater London spans 12 hexes (~300 sq miles). Features internal district partitioning and subterranean layers (Sewers/London Underground).
- **Time Controls & Day/Night Cycle:** Global `TickManager` supporting `0x` (Pause), `1x`, `2x`, `3x` and `5x` speeds (`Engine.time_scale`). Features a 40-minute real-time day/night cycle (20 minutes Day / 20 minutes Night at 1x speed).
- **Accessibility:** Every color-coded piece of state (biome, soil, Zone of Control, fog-of-war, building category, alerts) is paired with a distinct shape/icon, never color alone — cheap to hold to now, expensive to retrofit once more systems lean on color.
- **World State Framing:** Outside player-held ground, 1890 Britain is functionally dead — dark, unlit, and roamed by hordes with no fixed home (Phase 5.10). Player settlements are the only lit, defended points on the map (Phase 2.6's Fog of War and Phase 5.10's horde roaming are two views of the same idea — a beacon surrounded by darkness). The whole game arc runs from purely defensive "hold the light" play early-to-mid game toward proactively reclaiming and re-lighting the wider country late-game (Phase 5.11, Phase 7.4, Phase 7.5).

> Considered and declined, not merely forgotten — worth stating plainly so nobody re-litigates these mid-project. Revisit only if the game's commercial scope changes.
- **Multiplayer / co-op.** Single-player only; the persistent-background hook and the whole design (one player's colony) assumes it.

- **Mainland Europe.** Everything else in this doc — including Naval Logistics and Ireland (Phase 7.5) — ships in the initial release. Europe (Phase 7.7) is the one deliberate exception: a future paid expansion (~£5) if the game finds an audience, not built for v1.0.

## 🧩 Known Architecture Constraint: Hex-Grid Over-Coupling
> User feedback — **biomes now real sub-hex data (closed 2026-08-10), walls already real sub-hex geometry (closed earlier)**, but the pattern is broader than either alone and most of the list below is still genuinely open. Documented here so a future pass has a real, evidenced starting point instead of relitigating from scratch.

**Why this is a real constraint, not just a style nitpick:** one hex is ~25 sq. miles (`HexCell`'s own doc comment). Most fields below are still a SINGLE scalar value for that entire area — real terrain doesn't split cleanly into one uniform biome/soil/feature per 25-square-mile block, and it shows most at Tactical zoom (Phase 2.5), where the camera is close enough to see individual soldiers standing on ground.

**What's hex-locked today, concretely:**
- [x] **Biomes (closed 2026-08-10):** `HexCell.biome_type`/`soil_fertility`/`elevation` are STILL one scalar per hex (deliberately — see below), but that scalar is now DERIVED from real sub-hex data instead of a flat default. New `RealTerrainSampler.gd` samples two whole-map-aligned rasters (`assets/terrain_data/landcover.png`/`elevation.png`) baked offline from OpenStreetMap land-cover/water data + AWS/Mapzen SRTM-derived elevation (`tools/geo_bake/bake_landcover.py`, a real, committed, reproducible script — unlike the earlier coastline bake, whose own pipeline was never checked in). `HexMapGenerator._apply_real_terrain()` takes a 5x5 real-world sample plurality vote per hex instead of defaulting everything to Moorland. **The actual sub-hex payoff is at Tactical zoom**: new `SubHexGroundView.gd` replaces the old flat per-hex ground tile with a 7x7 grid of real biome/water/elevation samples, each rendered with the SAME existing terrain SVGs — a single hex can now visibly show a hillside corner, a river cutting through, and open farmland simultaneously, not one uniform tile. Deliberately does NOT let a minority of real river/canal sub-samples flip a hex's own `biome_type` scalar to WATERWAY — that would reintroduce hex-locking one layer down; real river geometry is the sub-hex renderer's job, not a hex-level rule. **Scope note**: the real bake currently covers only the actually-playable England corridor (Manchester/Midlands/London + margin), not Wales/Scotland/Ireland (hard-gated, zero authored content, Phase 7.2.1) — `RealTerrainSampler` gracefully falls back to the old flat default outside that coverage, so nothing regresses there; extending real coverage later is a re-run of the same script with a wider range.
- [x] **Walls (closed earlier, this constraint doc was stale about it):** `WallSegment.point_a`/`point_b` are real world-space line endpoints, not hex-edge-locked — freehand placement at any angle, auto-chopped into independent ≤100m pieces. `hex_a`/`hex_b` survive only as a spatial-index/ZoC-classification convenience, not the actual geometry.
- **Supply lines:** `SupplyLineSegment.hex_a`/`hex_b` — still the OLD shape walls used to have (`WallManager`'s own doc comment notes the parallel). A road/rail/canal is either "the whole edge" or doesn't exist. Not yet revisited.
- **Zone of Control, Noise, Fog of War:** `LogisticsNetwork`, `NoiseManager`, `FogOfWarManager` all still store/derive their coverage as a flat per-hex value (`Dictionary` keyed `Vector2i -> state`), radiating via `HexCoord.hex_disk()`. A hex is either VISIBLE or it isn't; either covered by ZoC or it isn't; either under noise pressure or it isn't — no gradient across the hex's own area. Not yet revisited.
- **Districts (`District.gd`/`DistrictPartitioner.gd`) are the one nominally "sub-hex" system that predates this pass, and it still doesn't actually solve this.** It's a fixed categorical template — a settlement hex always gets exactly "Urban Center + Industrial Estate (safe) + Uncleared Wilderness (contested)," a non-settlement hex always gets exactly one "fully contested wilderness" — with no position, shape, or real geography behind any of the three. It answers "is combat active somewhere in this hex," not "where."

**What's already NOT hex-locked, for contrast:** buildings/units' own `local_position` (Phase 2.5.6/2.5.7), walls' `point_a`/`point_b`, and now terrain's real sub-hex sample grid (this pass) — the *placement/presentation* and *terrain* layers have real sub-hex geometry. Supply lines, ZoC/Noise/Fog of War, and Districts are the genuinely remaining hex-locked systems.

- [ ] Revisit ZoC/Noise/Fog of War and supply lines if/when a dedicated sub-hex spatial partition gets designed for them too — Districts' `Array[District]` per hex is still the closest existing seam to extend, but that's a real design decision for that future pass, not decided here. Terrain's own fix (this pass) used a DIFFERENT approach (a sampled raster, not a Districts-style partition) since it's read-heavy/write-never data, not the same shape as ZoC/Districts' own contested-flag-flips-at-runtime need — don't assume the same technique transfers directly.

## 📐 Standing Directive: Design Against *They Are Billions*, Don't Invent New Mechanics
> User feedback, 2026-08-09, verbatim in spirit: "This is all built in They Are Billions but for some reason I'm having to spec it out again... you aren't basing these mechanics on what I am [referencing] and instead inventing your own mechanics." This project's own pitch (Technical Summary, Phase 2.5's own header) explicitly names *They Are Billions* as the reference for the country-scale-to-individual-soldier feel — that reference has NOT consistently been used as an actual mechanical source when building wall, siege, and movement systems specifically. **Before designing or redesigning wall placement/destruction, zombie movement/AI, or siege behavior, research how *They Are Billions* actually implements it first, and match that model** rather than deriving a new one from first principles. The two concrete cases logged below (wall segmentation, unit/zombie movement) are the first identified instances — treat this as a standing constraint on any future work touching either system, not a one-time fix.

## 🐞 Player-Reported Bugs (logged 2026-08-09) — ✅ All 8 Fixed 2026-08-10
> Reported directly by the user after playtesting the build produced by commits `82da243` and `e651877` (both same-day, both explicitly claiming to fix building/wall visibility, which turned out to still be wrong — see the first item below for the real root cause). Logged first per the user's explicit "log these bugs" request, then implemented and verified the following day — every item below carries its own verification method (live screenshot, headless self-test, or a live-tested "couldn't reproduce" investigation), not just a code-review claim. Two items (wall placement, wall segmentation) turned out to require real new systems, not tuning fixes — see their own entries for why.

- [x] **Buildings are still never visible on the map, at all — reported twice in the same message for emphasis.** **Real root cause found and FIXED, 2026-08-10, verified with an actual post-fix screenshot (not headless-only, per this entry's own prior instruction).** The prior two "fixes" were treating symptoms (camera position, box size, an outline) that were real but not the actual cause: `Polygon2D.uv` in Godot is texture-PIXEL-space, not normalized 0..1 — a well-known Godot gotcha (godotengine/godot#21830). Three places (`TacticalHexView`'s building box, `BuildPlacementController`'s ghost preview, `StrategicOverlayManager`'s Ditch/Oil Pit icon) all independently wrote a `(0,0)-(1,1)` "unit square" uv array, which only ever sampled a literal 1x1-pixel patch in each texture's top-left corner — landing on fully transparent background on every AI-generated building PNG, rendering an invisible fill with only the outline visible. Fixed with a new shared `TacticalHexView.quad_uv(texture)` (real pixel dimensions from `texture.get_size()`), used by all three call sites. **New follow-up found while verifying, NOT the same bug**: the AI-generated building PNGs have an imperfect alpha matte — a large, mostly-opaque dark halo around the actual illustration that only becomes genuinely transparent very close to the canvas edge (confirmed via a headless `Image.get_pixel()` sample: (500,500) on `town_hall.png` is alpha=1.0, not background) — renders as a dark box behind each now-visible building. Cosmetic, not a visibility blocker (the building itself is clearly legible) — needs either regenerated art with a clean alpha cutout or a careful automated alpha-cleanup pass; not fixed this pass.
- [x] **Garrisons cannot be placed anywhere, ever.** **Investigated live, 2026-08-10 — could NOT reproduce a Garrison-specific defect; placement, validation, resource cost, and rejection messaging all work correctly.** Live test: selected Garrison, clicked Manchester's own settlement hex (URBAN + `is_settlement`) — the ghost preview showed a green (valid) hex outline, click succeeded, resources deducted exactly as costed (Bricks 100→10, Cast Iron 40→15), and a toast read "Garrison under construction — ready in 3 days." Tried a second Garrison on the adjacent settlement hex immediately after — correctly rejected with a clear, ACCURATE toast ("Not enough resources to build Garrison," since the first Garrison had already spent the Bricks/Cast Iron down below the second one's cost) rather than a mysterious failure. **Most likely explanation for the original report**: this is very probably a PERCEIVED symptom of the buildings-invisible bug above (now fixed), not a separate defect — a Garrison placed successfully and correctly queued 3 days of construction, but with buildings never rendering, the player would have had zero way to ever confirm it actually appeared, indistinguishable from "placement does nothing." Combined with `requires_settlement` gating Housing & Civil buildings to the single small pre-authored Manchester hex cluster (genuinely easy to miss/misclick away from, though every other click destination correctly explains itself via the same toast, e.g. "Garrison can only be built within a settlement."), this fully accounts for the reported experience without any placement-logic bug existing. **Real, disclosed but NOT fixed this pass**: there is still no player-driven way to ever found a second settlement/urbanize a new hex (`is_settlement`+`URBAN` are only ever set once, at map-gen, in `HexMapGenerator._apply_feature()`'s `SETTLEMENT` branch) — a legitimate future design question (should territory expansion ever unlock new Housing & Civil ground?), not something to invent a fix for under a "bug fix" pass without the user weighing in.
- [x] **Wall segments are hex-edge-locked (one segment = one full hex edge, ~5 real miles at this project's own ~25 sq mi/hex scale) and destroy as one monolithic piece.** **Fixed 2026-08-10, verified live end-to-end (screenshots).** A deeper finding first: a project-wide grep found `WallManager.place_segment()` had ZERO call sites anywhere outside its own file except `seed_starting_defenses()` — there was never ANY player-facing wall placement UI at all, freehand or otherwise; every wall a player has ever seen was the free starting perimeter. Reworked properly rather than patched: `WallSegment` gained real `point_a`/`point_b` world-space endpoints (its actual geometry now — `hex_a`/`hex_b` survive only as a spatial-index/ZoC-classification convenience). New `WallManager.place_wall_line(world_a, world_b, tier, is_gate)` chops any drawn line into independently-placed, independently-HP'd pieces no longer than `WallCatalog.MAX_SEGMENT_LENGTH_WORLD_UNITS` (100m, per `HexCoord.WORLD_UNITS_PER_REAL_METER`'s real-scale conversion — also newly shared with `TacticalHexView.BUILDING_HALF_SIZE`'s own derivation). Cost scales down with each piece's own length (a full-length drawn wall costs about what the old one-piece-per-edge wall used to, just spread across many cheap pieces) but **max HP does NOT scale down** — confirmed against real *They Are Billions* mechanics via direct research: each small piece is fully as tough as its tier implies, so a long wall's TOTAL toughness genuinely goes up with how much of it you build, the real fix for "one hit wipes 5 miles." `HordeManager`'s siege-blocking replaced `get_segment_between(hex_a, hex_b)` with new `WallManager.get_blocking_segment()` (real line-vs-line intersection via `Geometry2D.segment_intersects_segment()` against the horde's actual travel path, not a hex-pair lookup) — the core "walls block hordes" mechanic verified to still route through unchanged logic, just against the right geometry now. **Built the missing placement UI from scratch**: new `WallPlacementController.gd` (click-drag along the map, genuinely freehand — not hex-snapped, matching the user's literal ask even though real *They Are Billions* itself turned out to be grid-snapped-per-tile, not arbitrary-angle, per the research) plus a new "Walls" tab in `BuildMenuView` (Wooden Wall / Gate buttons). Live-verified: dragged a wall at a real arbitrary diagonal angle across open hexes (green valid-placement preview tracking the cursor) → released → a solid piece-chain rendered along that exact angle, Wood spent (scaled cost, not the old flat rate) → clicked partway along it → selected exactly one small piece (`Wall segment / Wooden Wall — HP 100/100`, a highlight covering only that piece, not the whole drawn line) via the repair-selection UI from the previous fix. Every part of the original complaint (hex-locked, not freehand, whole-edge destruction) is closed.
- [x] **No way to select a placed wall or building to repair it — the repair functions are unreachable dead code from the UI today.** **Fixed 2026-08-10, verified live end-to-end (screenshots) for both buildings and walls, including an actual repair completing.** `UnitCommandController._select_at()` now hit-tests ANY building on the clicked hex (not just training ones — picked by proximity to the exact click when several share a hex via `local_position`) and, before that, checks whether the click landed within `_WALL_CLICK_TOLERANCE` world units of a wall segment's own line (`hex_a` center to `hex_b` center) — a genuine first, walls had zero selection path before this. New `building_instance_selected(instance)`/`wall_segment_selected(segment)` signals (replacing the old hex-only `building_selected(coord)`) carry the real object, plus a gold selection ring (buildings) / highlighted line (walls) so it's visually obvious what's selected. `UnitPanelView` now renders a building-info or wall-info panel (name/tier/HP/ruined-or-breached state) with a **Repair button that only appears once the thing is actually damaged**, disabled with the real rejection reason as a tooltip otherwise — wired straight to new `UnitCommandController.repair_selected_building()`/`repair_selected_wall()` wrappers. Live-tested: selected an undamaged wall (no Repair button, correct) → selected Cast Iron Foundry vs. Town Hall on the same hex (correctly disambiguated by click proximity) → placed a Garrison, fast-forwarded time to let it finish, selected it (correctly showed BOTH the repair section AND its Train buttons together) → a horde breached a wall segment mid-test (unplanned, useful) → selected the now-red breached segment → Repair button appeared, enabled → clicked it → "Repairing wall segment — ready in 1 day" toast fired and Wood was actually spent. Every piece of the reported gap is closed.
- [x] **Map terrain props with a canonical "right way up" (trees, etc.) spawn at fully random rotation, so many render upside-down.** **Fixed 2026-08-10** — `LocalDetailGenerator.gd` now has `_FREE_ROTATION_PROP_TYPES` (only `ROCK`, the one prop with no real-world up/down) and gives every other type (TREE/BUSH/REED, all real illustrated things with a canopy/growth-direction "up", confirmed directly against `tree.png`'s own art) a small `±20°` sway jitter instead of a full `0..TAU` spin. Code-fixed and reasoned through directly against the real art, but **not separately screenshot-verified this pass** (ran out of a working in-session camera-pan repro after the building fix — the pan keypresses stopped reaching the game window; unresolved, not investigated further this pass) — worth an explicit live check next time props are touched.
- [x] **Zombies and units don't move organically — they beeline dead-straight to a hex's exact center, then pivot, in a visibly programmatic way, instead of wandering like something alive reacting to stimuli.** **Fixed 2026-08-10.** Research first, per the standing directive above: *They Are Billions*' own idle zombies are documented as mostly near-stationary punctuated by short investigative moves rather than following fixed routes, and — key finding — hex/tile SELECTION already had real stimulus-reaction in this project (`HordeManager`'s `WANDERING`/`ATTRACTED` state machine, Phase 5.2/5.10, noise-driven, already built and not the bug); the actual gap was purely the leg-by-leg MOTION underneath always being a ruler-straight line to the exact hex center, plus the shared figure-formation code (`TacticalEntityLayer._scatter_offset()` — confirmed used by BOTH unit squads and zombie hordes, `angle := TAU*index/total`) placing every figure at perfectly even ring angles, the literal "rotate around it in a programmatic way" being described.
  - `MovementStepper.advance_toward_hex()` now applies a small perpendicular wobble (new `wobble_seed` param, caller passes the Horde/UnitInstance's own `.id` so different entities don't sway in lockstep) to the direction vector during a step — but **only ever on the bounded partial-step branch**; a whole-leg commit (a multi-hex `TickManager` catch-up burst) still beelines exactly to the target hex center, zero wobble, preserving the wall-crossing safety invariant this exact code review session already fixed (`HordeManager`'s per-crossing wall check re-runs fresh from the entity's real position every call regardless). **Verified empirically, not just by inspection** — a headless self-test confirmed: exact arrival every time (`local_position` still lands precisely on `Vector2.ZERO`), a real measured max deviation of ~24 world units over an ~887-unit leg (2.7%, tuned down from an initial 0.45 rad pass that measured ~65 units/7% — too wide, felt like a snake-weave rather than organic drift), different `wobble_seed` values genuinely producing different paths, and — the safety-critical case — a huge single-frame catch-up burst still landing exactly on the target hex with zero wobble residue.
  - `TacticalEntityLayer._scatter_offset()` (shared by unit squads AND zombie clusters) replaced the perfect ring with a deterministic-but-irregular scatter (both angle and radius jitter via a cheap positional hash), seeded per-group (`instance.id`/`horde.id`) so different squads/hordes don't all share one identical shape.
  - **Honest disclosure**: the movement-safety math got real empirical verification (headless self-test, not just code review) given the stakes (this touches the exact mechanic the wall rework above depends on) — the figure-scatter visual result specifically was NOT separately screenshotted this pass (a live verification attempt landed on the wrong panel mid-session and wasn't re-attempted given the lower risk of that specific change); worth a quick live look next time either system is touched.
- [x] **Buildings aren't sized against the real ~5mi×5mi hex scale.** **Fixed 2026-08-10** — `TacticalHexView.BUILDING_HALF_SIZE` is now derived from `HexCoord.HEX_SIZE`'s real-world span (a hex's real circumradius, solved from its own documented ~25 sq mi area) and a representative 100m×100m building footprint, landing at `5.128` world units (down from the old arbitrary `70.0`) — full derivation in that constant's own doc comment. `ObstacleRadii.BUILDING_RADIUS` (steering clearance, had already silently drifted out of sync across TWO prior building-box resizes before this one) re-synced to match. Verified visually in the same post-fix screenshot as the visibility fix above — building now reads as a correctly small, detailed structure rather than dominating the hex.

## 📝 Phase 1: Engine Foundation, Terrain & Geography — ✅ Built
Godot 4.x project scaffolded (`/scripts`, `/scenes`, `/assets`), background-execution-friendly display settings, and `CameraController.gd`'s dual TOP_DOWN/ISOMETRIC toggle (a "faked" 2D rotate-45°-and-squash-Y trick over the flat world, not true isometric art — see the Architecture Constraint section above for how this interacts with unit art).

**Hex map & real geography (`HexGridMap.gd`, `BritishGeographyData.gd`):** the whole UK+Ireland landmass, baked from Natural Earth's public-domain 10m coastline GeoJSON (not hand-authored), recalibrated to a genuine ~25 sq mi/hex scale — `MAP_BOUNDS` is 154×179 (27,566 hexes), ~4,692 land. `GameEnums.BiomeType.OCEAN` is impassable and skips `HexCellView` spawn entirely for performance (~1.6s full headless boot). Named settlements/features (Manchester, Birmingham, London, Pennines, Chilterns, Thames/Mersey/Trent, Chat Moss, the Fens, etc.) project from real lon/lat via an equirectangular pipeline, snapped to nearest land hex where needed. Wales/Scotland/Ireland have landmass but no authored content yet (locked/inaccessible, Phase 7.2.1/7.4/7.5). `CoastlineOutlineView.gd` draws the land/ocean boundary even through unexplored Fog of War — public geography, not a gameplay secret. **Known, disclosed risk:** the full map span (~214,600×136,700 world units) is past the ~100,000-unit range where single-precision float jitter typically starts (`HexCoord.HEX_SIZE`'s own doc comment) — a floating-origin camera rebase would be the real fix, not attempted; live screenshot testing has shown no visible jitter so far.

Granular soil fertility (`HexCell.soil_fertility`) and District partitioning (`District.gd`/`DistrictPartitioner.gd` — Urban Center/Industrial Estate/Uncleared Wilderness per settlement hex) both implemented; see the Architecture Constraint section above for the real limits of both.

- [ ] **Ambient ruins from real settlement data (user request, 2026-08-11) — scoped, not built this pass.** Distinct from Phase 5.12's existing `BuildingInstance.is_ruined` mechanic (that's player-BUILT-then-destroyed structures only) — this is pre-existing rubble scattered across unowned/uncontrolled land, based on where real UK settlements actually exist. **Why not built in the same pass as the WOODLAND/HEATHLAND terrain-granularity work above:** `tools/geo_bake/fetch_overpass.py`'s query currently pulls only `landuse`/`natural`/`waterway` tags — zero `building=*` data, confirmed by direct grep. ## ⚔️ Phase 5: Threat Mechanics, Day/Night & Horde Intel — ✅ Built (core), Phase 5.11 not started
The single largest system in the project. `TimeCycleManager.gd` (autoload after `TickManager`) derives Day/Night from `TickManager.get_day_progress()`.

(2) the batched QuadMesh was a fixed square with no aspect-ratio correction, unlike every other sprite-drawing function in the same file (`_build_unit_figure()`/`_build_role_marker()`/`_build_zombie_figure()` all preserve aspect ratio via the texture's own largest dimension) — currently inert since all 3 shipped `zombie_%d.png` assets are genuine 2048x2048 squares and the art pipeline's own README mandates 1:1 for every past and future asset in the category, but a real code-level gap now fixed to match its siblings rather than relying on the art convention to keep bailing it out. (3) `_rebuild_zombie_batches()` used to unconditionally recompute every figure's scatter offset and re-upload every transform every single frame, even for a horde provably frozen in render-space (`stun_seconds_remaining > 0`, or `ATTACKING` an unbreached wall — a siege that can grind on for many ticks) — fixed with a per-frame signature (id/hex_coord/local_position/size/fog-visibility per horde) compared against last frame's; an unchanged signature skips the whole per-figure recompute (MultiMesh instance buffers persist on the GPU on their own — nothing needs re-uploading to keep showing what's already there). **Verified via 26 headless self-test assertions across two passes** (boundary conditions on the far/near classification, `_replan_cheap()`'s neighbor validity, `_replan()` end-to-end far-vs-near routing, MultiMesh instance-count capping/fidelity-zeroing, the high-speed multi-hex-per-frame fix, the dirty-check skip/re-trigger, batch-layer `z_index`) plus live screenshots (a 500-strong test horde rendering hundreds of individual figures correctly through the batched pipeline, no visual corruption, before AND after the adversarial-review fixes) — all passed, zero regressions. **Disclosed, not solved this pass** (all from the same review, none blocking): a reloaded `ATTACKING` horde whose wall sits farther than `FAR_SIMULATION_RADIUS` from every building loses some of its old odds of naturally re-routing back to the same wall (the underlying "reload always loses the ATTACKING *label*" gap predates this pass — `_replan()` has never read `state` before picking a target — this only narrows one input to it, and neither path was ever wall-aware); the far/near classification has no hysteresis, so a horde lingering right at the boundary can genuinely alternate cheap/real replans (confirmed via simulation — real, harmless, purely cosmetic); the aggregate render cap's greedy-first-come allocation isn't stable frame-to-frame under horde-population churn once total zombies actually exceed 5,000 (unlikely at today's real starting-horde economy, exactly the scale this pass is built toward).
