> **Project Title:** `The Smog & The Swarm`
> **Project Code:** `SMOG-SWARM-1890`  
> **Github Url:** https://github.com/LimtlessLtd/Smog-Swarm-1890
> **Engine & Language:** Godot Engine 4.x (GDScript)

## 🏛️ Project Architecture & Design Overview

- **Core Loop:** Continuous persistent expansion across Victorian Britain hex sectors. Build defenses, automate patrols, manage daily resource upkeeps (Food/Energy/Gunpowder), and protect supply lines while working in the background.
- **Aesthetic Direction:** Grounded late-18th/19th-century Industrial Revolution (brick, cast iron, coal smoke, authentic Victorian architecture)—explicitly **no** retro-futuristic steampunk tropes. Applies even to the top-tier "mechanized" units in Phase 5.4 — heavy steam engineering (armoured traction engines, rail guns, breaching cranes), never a humanoid battle-mech.
- **Dual Perspective Engine:** Dynamic camera toggle between **Top-Down Orthographic** (tactical layout) and **2.5D Isometric** (cinematic viewing).
- **Multi-Layer Macro-Hex System:** Continuous tilemap hiding an underlying axial hex grid `(q, r)`. Each hex represents a 5x5 mile macro-region (~25 sq miles). Cities scale dynamically across multiple macro-tiles—Manchester spans 4 hexes (~100 sq miles), while Greater London spans 12 hexes (~300 sq miles). Features internal district partitioning and subterranean layers (Sewers/London Underground).
- **Time Controls & Day/Night Cycle:** Global `TickManager` supporting `0x` (Pause), `1x`, `2x`, `3x` and `5x` speeds (`Engine.time_scale`). Features a 40-minute real-time day/night cycle (20 minutes Day / 20 minutes Night at 1x speed).
- **Accessibility:** Every color-coded piece of state (biome, soil, Zone of Control, fog-of-war, building category, alerts) is paired with a distinct shape/icon, never color alone — cheap to hold to now, expensive to retrofit once more systems lean on color.
- **World State Framing:** Outside player-held ground, 1890 Britain is functionally dead — dark, unlit, and roamed by hordes with no fixed home (Phase 5.10). Player settlements are the only lit, defended points on the map (Phase 2.6's Fog of War and Phase 5.10's horde roaming are two views of the same idea — a beacon surrounded by darkness). The whole game arc runs from purely defensive "hold the light" play early-to-mid game toward proactively reclaiming and re-lighting the wider country late-game (Phase 5.11, Phase 7.4, Phase 7.5).

## 🚫 Explicitly Out of Scope
> Considered and declined, not merely forgotten — worth stating plainly so nobody re-litigates these mid-project. Revisit only if the game's commercial scope changes.
- **Multiplayer / co-op.** Single-player only; the persistent-background hook and the whole design (one player's colony) assumes it.
- **Steam Workshop / mod support.** Not for initial release — code-drawn placeholder visuals and data-driven building/tech definitions would make this plausible later, but it's not being built for now.
- **Rival human factions / diplomacy.** The threat is zombies (plus the alien-origin mystery); no competing AI colonies or negotiation systems.
- **Full localization.** English only for initial release.
- **Mainland Europe.** Everything else in this doc — including Naval Logistics and Ireland (Phase 7.5) — ships in the initial release. Europe (Phase 7.7) is the one deliberate exception: a future paid expansion (~£5) if the game finds an audience, not built for v1.0.

## 🔎 Phase 2.5: Tactical Zoom & Local Detail Layer
> Added mid-development (not in the original spec) to guarantee the intended *They Are Billions*-at-country-scale feel: seamlessly zoom from a large chunk of Great Britain down to individual trees and buildings. Built now, ahead of Phase 3, because Phase 4's freeform wall-snapping already needs precise coordinate placement rather than hex-granularity — this is the foundation that unlocks that, not a detour from it.
> **Major expansion from a grilling session (`/grill-me`)**, working through what "zoom to micro level" actually needs to mean once Phase 5's units/hordes/combat existed to zoom in ON — 2.5.1-2.5.3 proved the two-tier camera cut and gave buildings exact placement, 2.5.6/2.5.7/2.5.4/2.5.5 (in that build order) then delivered true-scale in-hex coordinates, zoom-gated placement, individual unit/squad/zombie rendering, and the LOD bands on top of it — see each entry below. **The entire grilled-out Phase 2.5 expansion is now implemented.** Phase 2.12 covers the terrain-movement/vision half of that same conversation, split into its own phase rather than bundled in here (decided — see that phase's own header).
- [x] **2.5.1 Two-Tier Map Rendering (`CameraController.gd`, `LocalDetailManager.gd`)**
  - [x] Hard zoom-threshold switch between the abstract Strategic hex view (the "large chunk of the UK" view) and a continuous, coordinate-precise Tactical view of a single hex's ~5x5 mile footprint — a pure camera zoom crossing a line, no loading screen or separate map.
  - [x] Tactical detail only generates for settled/frontier hexes (settlement, has a placed building, or covered by Military/Civilian Zone of Control) — distant unclaimed wilderness stays an abstract tile even at max zoom, keeping the persistent background simulation cheap at full-country scale.
  - [x] A neighborhood of qualifying hexes around the camera hydrates/dehydrates as the player pans while zoomed in, so tactical detail follows a contiguous developed territory rather than a single fixed hex.
- [x] **2.5.2 Procedural Local Detail (`LocalDetailGenerator.gd`)**
  - [x] Deterministic, coordinate-seeded prop scatter (trees/bushes/rocks/reeds) varied by each hex's biome — regenerated on demand instead of authored/saved per hex.
- [x] **2.5.3 Precise Building Placement**
  - [x] BuildingInstance/BuildingManager extended with an exact position within a hex (`place_building_at_world()`), instead of only a hex-coordinate slot, so buildings render at real positions in the Tactical view.
- [x] **2.5.4 Individual Units & Squads at Tactical Zoom** — implemented. `HexCoord.entry_local_position(from_coord, to_coord)` (new) extends `BuildingInstance.local_position`'s own pattern (Phase 2.5.3) to `UnitInstance`/`Horde`: both gained a `local_position` field, written by `UnitOrderController._advance_toward()`/`HordeManager._advance_horde()` on every hex step — positions the entity toward the edge it walked in from instead of the hex-center jump they used to do. `UnitSaveEntry`/`UnitManager` extended to persist it for units; `Horde` saves it for free since the whole Resource is already saved directly.
  - [x] **Rendering splits by tier, not role**, as decided. New `UnitInstance.get_squad_headcount()`/`is_squad_rendered()` (`definition.tier <= 3`) plus new `scripts/world/TacticalEntityLayer.gd` (a `WorldRoot` sibling, visible only at Tactical zoom): Tier 0-3 units draw as a ring of `SQUAD_SIZE` (5) code-drawn figures around their own `local_position`; Tier 4-5 draw as one larger single model instead. Zombies get the same individual-figure treatment too — every `Horde` draws a capped cluster of figures (`MAX_RENDERED_ZOMBIES` = 30, a much smaller placeholder than Phase 5.10's own future `~5,000-100,000` GPU-batched cap, since this is a naive one-`Polygon2D`-per-figure renderer, not that future `MultiMeshInstance2D` system) — fog-gated on `FogOfWarManager.is_visible()`, a stricter live-vision bar than `TacticalHexView`'s own at-least-`EXPLORED` requirement for terrain. **Decided (this class):** deliberately NOT gated on `LocalDetailManager`'s hex-hydration set the way props/buildings are — a unit or horde crossing an otherwise-abstract wilderness hex still needs to render while the camera is looking straight at it in Tactical zoom; before this class existed, `StrategicOverlayManager`'s own markers simply vanished in Tactical mode (`visible = not is_tactical`) with nothing replacing them, so every unit/horde was literally invisible at Tactical zoom. Headcount/figure-count is polled fresh every frame from `UnitManager.get_all_units()`/`HordeManager.get_all_hordes()` (self-healing, same diff-and-reconcile shape `LocalDetailManager` already uses for its own camera-driven neighborhood) rather than wired through every individual signal that could change `current_hp`/`size` — only redraws a group's figures when its derived count actually changed.
  - [x] **Squad headcount is derived, not new stored state**, as decided: `ceil(current_hp / (max_hp / SQUAD_SIZE))`, clamped to at least 1 while alive and at most `SQUAD_SIZE`. Fixed squad size regardless of individual unit stats, per the decision; scaling by tier/role remains a future balancing pass.
  - [x] **A fallen squad member becomes a real, fightable threat** — implemented exactly as decided, extending Phase 5.9's casualty pipeline. `CombatCoordinator._engage()` snapshots `get_squad_headcount()` before and after `CombatEngine.resolve_engagement()` and calls `HordeManager.add_casualty_zombies(unit.hex_coord, figures_lost)` for however many headcount points that one engagement cost — covers both a single mid-fight loss and a multi-figure loss from one big hit in the same call, and subsumes the old "only on total unit loss" trigger as its own last-figure special case (a Tier 4-5 vehicle, headcount always 1 while alive, still only ever fires once, on its own death — a strict generalization, not a behavior change for those). The resulting `Horde` is resolved by the already-built `CombatCoordinator` contact system with zero new combat code — it fights whoever's on that hex next, naturally including its own former squadmates first. Verified via a temporary headless self-test hook (reverted before commit, same pattern as prior phases): a one-shot-kill engagement spawns a casualty horde sized to the unit's full squad (5), and a calibrated partial hit that drops headcount 5→3 without destroying the unit spawns a casualty horde of exactly 2 — the mid-fight case, not just total loss.
  - [x] **Healing/replenishment is Garrison-gated**, as decided — the project's first regen mechanic of any kind. New `UnitOrderController._regen_if_friendly()`, called from the `HOLD`/`GARRISON` branch of the existing movement tick (previously a no-op `pass`): heals `GARRISON_REGEN_FRACTION_PER_TICK` (5%, placeholder balancing number) of `max_hp` per tick while the unit's current hex carries Military or Civilian Zone of Control coverage (reusing the already-wired, previously pathfinding-only `logistics_network_path` reference — no new export needed), doing nothing mid-patrol or on unclaimed ground. Squad headcount regrowth needed no extra code — it's already derived live from `current_hp` everywhere it's read. Verified in the same self-test hook: a wounded `GARRISON` unit on the seeded starting Town Hall's hex heals, an identically-wounded `HOLD` unit on unclaimed ground does not.
  - [x] Zombies get the same individual-figure treatment at Tactical zoom, including zombies not currently part of any horde — covered by `TacticalEntityLayer` above (every `Horde`, regardless of size, gets a figure cluster). The "lone zombie" spawn-tuning, merge/split, and Strategic-marker-threshold decisions remain Phase 5.10's own still-unbuilt entries, exactly as scoped there — this phase only owns the rendering.
- [x] **2.5.5 Multi-Tier Visual Fidelity (LOD)** — implemented. Four distinct zoom tiers, not the original two, as decided. Strategic view's icon-based rendering (`StrategicOverlayManager`) is genuinely untouched — it doesn't consult fidelity at all. Tactical view (past the existing `tactical_zoom_threshold`) gets three internal fidelity bands as the camera keeps zooming in toward `max_zoom` (corrected from this bullet's own stale `min_zoom` reference, a leftover from before 2.5.6's zoom-direction bug fix — `max_zoom` is the zoomed-IN bound). New `GameEnums.TacticalFidelity` (LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH) + `CameraController.get_tactical_fidelity()`/`medium_fidelity_threshold`/`high_fidelity_threshold`/`tactical_fidelity_changed` (evenly divide `[tactical_zoom_threshold, max_zoom]` into thirds — exact values are the balancing pass the last bullet below already called out, not decided precisely here) drive both consumers:
  - [x] **Low fidelity:** implemented — simple silhouettes/blobs, shape-and-color differentiated. `TacticalEntityLayer` (Phase 2.5.4) draws exactly one uniform circle blob per unit (any role/tier) and one DIAMOND blob per horde (any size) — reusing the same diamond shape `StrategicOverlayManager`'s own horde marker already uses at Strategic zoom (2.7.6), so "unit vs zombie" reads by shape, not color alone, even at the lowest Tactical band. `TacticalHexView` collapses every prop's per-species polygon (tree/bush/rock/reed) to one uniform blob shape, still colored by prop type. **Decided: buildings are NOT simplified at any fidelity** — their category-color box is already the simplest shape that still carries "which building category is this," the exact cue this tier's own "tell unit from building from zombie" bar depends on; simplifying it further would remove that cue rather than reduce needless detail.
  - [x] **Medium fidelity:** implemented — `TacticalEntityLayer` draws one marker per unit shaped by role (circle/triangle/diamond — melee/ranged/special) and sized by tier, and a small FIXED-size zombie cluster per horde (`MEDIUM_ZOMBIE_CLUSTER_SIZE` = 5, deliberately not the true headcount) — genuinely discernible role/tier detail, deliberately short of individual-figure detail. `TacticalHexView`'s props/buildings render identically to High at this tier (see the cross-reference note below).
  - [x] **High fidelity:** unchanged from Phase 2.5.4 — individual squad figures/zombies, today's real per-species prop polygons and building boxes. **Scope decision made while implementing:** the design doc's own "elaborate code-drawn buildings/props" line for this tier turned out to have no real Medium-vs-High distinction to build without real art (Phase 6.3) to make High more elaborate than Medium — buildings/props render identically at Medium and High; only Low (the simplification above) is a genuinely different rendering path for them. The design doc's own tier text only actually calls out a Medium-vs-High distinction for UNITS, not props/buildings, so this isn't a shortfall against what was decided, just a clarification of what "elaborate" resolved to.
  - [x] Exact zoom-value thresholds between the three bands are a balancing/feel pass, as decided — `CameraController.medium_fidelity_threshold`/`high_fidelity_threshold` (new) are real, tunable exports, currently just an even three-way split of the existing Tactical range.
- [x] **2.5.6 True-Scale In-Hex Coordinates** — implemented. **Decided (an easy-to-miss constraint from the same conversation, called out explicitly so 2.5.4/2.5.5 don't get built without it):** a hex is a real 5x5 mile, 25-square-mile area (design doc header, Phase 1.3) — huge. `HexCoord.HEX_SIZE` (was 64.0, its own doc comment already flagged this as "a placeholder visual scale until real art defines tile dimensions") squeezed that entire 25 square miles into one small, fixed-size rendered footprint regardless of zoom level — fine for a building-scale Tactical view, nowhere near enough room for "pan around and find individually-visible soldiers somewhere across a real 25-square-mile area." **Chosen approach: a straight 8x world-space scale multiplier (`HEX_SIZE` 64.0 -> 512.0) rather than a dual-coordinate-space/floating-origin rearchitecture** — every system that places or reads a world position already goes through `HexCoord.axial_to_world()`/`world_to_axial()`, so a single constant change keeps Strategic click/render math, the minimap, fog of war, and every marker system automatically consistent for free; verified safe against float precision (the full 40x28 hex map now spans ~48,000x21,500 world units, comfortably under the ~100,000-unit range where single-precision jitter typically starts). `CameraController`'s zoom-related constants and `Main.tscn`'s starting camera zoom were scaled by 1/8 alongside this (visible world width is `viewport/zoom.x`, so a bigger world needs smaller zoom values to frame the same fraction of it) and `pan_speed` by 8²=64x to compensate, preserving the exact same Strategic-view experience — same relative pan speed, same fraction of the map visible at a given zoom — despite the bigger world-space numbers underneath.
  - [x] High-fidelity Tactical zoom needs a genuinely large in-hex space to pan across — like panning the wider Strategic map already works, just one level down — not a small diorama that gets pixel-doubled as you zoom past it. The camera's own pan (unbounded, zoom-relative) and zoom-deep-within-Tactical range needed zero code changes beyond the constant rescale above — panning across the now-8x-bigger hex "just works."
  - [x] `LocalDetailGenerator`'s prop density (Phase 2.5.2) was tuned for a small fixed footprint; **chosen: scale prop count up to match** (the simpler of the two options this bullet left open) — bumped ~5x (14/6/8/10 -> 70/30/40/50) rather than a full area-matched rescale (which would mean several hundred props per hex) to keep the per-hex Polygon2D node count reasonable across the up-to-7 hexes `LocalDetailManager` can hydrate at once; `_SCATTER_RADIUS` itself already scales automatically since it's HEX_SIZE-relative.
  - [x] `BuildingInstance.local_position`/`UnitInstance.local_position`/`Horde.local_position` (the latter two landed with 2.5.4) keep meaning exactly what they mean today — an offset from hex center — just within a much larger valid range now that the hex's own local space is bigger. No save-format change: these are already plain `Vector2` fields (Phase 2.5.3).
  - [x] **Bug found and fixed while building this:** `CameraController`'s zoom-direction semantics were inverted from actual Godot `Camera2D` behavior (verified empirically — visible world width is `viewport/zoom.x`, not `viewport*zoom.x`) — scrolling "up" used to zoom the camera out, and `is_tactical_zoom()` used to trigger Tactical mode when zoomed further OUT rather than in. Fixed as its own separate commit/PR before layering this phase's scaling on top: flipped the comparison direction, repositioned `tactical_zoom_threshold` to the zoomed-in side of the default view, swapped the wheel-up/wheel-down mapping, and fixed the same inverted formula in `MinimapView`'s camera-viewport-frame indicator. Pre-existing, unrelated to this phase's own scope, but directly in the way of correctly reasoning about it.
- [x] **2.5.7 Placement Restricted to Tactical Zoom** — implemented. **Decided:** buildings AND wall segments (Phase 4.1 — no placement UI exists for walls yet regardless, so nothing to change there, just to build correctly from the start) can only be placed while zoomed into Tactical view, at any depth within it — reusing the existing `tactical_zoom_threshold`, not a stricter dedicated one; Strategic zoom simply isn't "in Tactical view" at all, nothing further to gate. Unit training is explicitly exempt — stays reachable from both Strategic and Tactical view, same as today, since training a unit at a Garrison doesn't place new precise geometry the way a building/wall does (unchanged — `UnitCommandController` never gated on zoom).
  - [x] `BuildMenuView`/`BuildPlacementController` (Phase 6.1) stay visible and clickable while zoomed out — selecting a building type while not in Tactical view is simply inert (no ghost preview, no placement on click) rather than the menu itself being hidden or the camera auto-zooming out from under the player. `BuildPlacementController.begin_placement()` still arms `_is_placing`/`_pending_type` at any zoom (so zooming in mid-selection just works), but ghost visibility and `_attempt_placement()` both gate on the new `_is_tactical_zoom()` (wraps `CameraController.is_tactical_zoom()`, wired via a new `camera_path` export, same NodePath pattern `LocalDetailManager`/`StrategicOverlayManager` already use) and live-update off `CameraController.tactical_mode_changed` as the player zooms in/out mid-selection. Feedback is a synthesized negative tone (new `scripts/audio/AlertTones.gd`, a stateless `RefCounted` utility generating a two-note descending square-wave buzz as a runtime `AudioStreamWAV` — no asset file, same "code-drawn placeholder" convention as every `*Visuals.gd` color lookup) played through a plain `AudioStreamPlayer` child — this is the project's first audio of any kind. The same tone now also plays off `BuildingManager.placement_rejected` for every pre-existing rejection reason (occupied hex, insufficient resources, wrong biome, etc.), not just the new zoom case. Verified via a temporary headless self-test hook (same pattern as prior phases): ghost hidden + click inert at Strategic zoom, ghost visible + click places at Tactical zoom, and the ghost appearing live when zoom crosses the threshold mid-selection — all 8 assertions passed; reverted before commit.

## 🌫️ Phase 2.6: Fog of War
> **2.6.1-2.6.4 implemented.** Documented as a prerequisite for Phase 5.1's "Fog of war contracts outside of Gas Streetlamps and Watchtower searchlights" (that line needs a fog system to contract in the first place), and because Phase 2.5's Tactical view needed a fog gate too — an unexplored hex has nothing known to render, at any zoom. 2.6.4 now runs on Phase 5.1's `TimeCycleManager`, which exists.
- [x] **2.6.1 Three-State Visibility Model (`GameEnums.FogState`, `FogOfWarManager.gd`)**
  - [x] `UNSEEN` (never scouted, rendered as blank darkness) → `EXPLORED` (terrain/buildings remembered, no current intel on movement) → `VISIBLE` (full real-time information). `UNSEEN → EXPLORED` is one-way per hex forever; `EXPLORED ↔ VISIBLE` toggles freely as vision sources come and go.
  - [x] `FogOfWarManager` (new system, wired as a Main.tscn sibling of LogisticsNetwork/BuildingManager rather than under WorldRoot — it never spawns its own positioned Node2D children, it only pushes state into HexGridMap's existing HexCellViews) owns fog state per hex and recomputes `VISIBLE` coverage whenever a vision source changes: building placed/removed (`BuildingManager`), Zone of Control recomputed (`LogisticsNetwork`). Unit movement (Phase 5.4) still pending, see 2.6.2.
  - [x] **Decided, implemented:** losing a hex's last vision source doesn't drop it to `EXPLORED` instantly — it lingers `VISIBLE` for `LOST_VISION_GRACE_SECONDS` (3s) first, so losing sight of a threat isn't instant/twitchy. A per-hex countdown in `_grace_remaining`, cancelled if the hex regains a vision source before it expires.
- [x] **2.6.2 Vision Sources**
  - [x] `BuildingDefinition.vision_radius` (new field, default 0 = "at least its own hex") — every placed building is a baseline vision source over its own hex; Gas Streetlamp (radius 1) and Church Steeple Watchtower (radius 2) project further, independent of whether they also project a Zone of Control.
  - [x] Military and Civilian ZoC coverage (`LogisticsNetwork.get_covered_hexes()`, new) double as vision sources — this was already the original Phase 2.3 spec's intent ("Military ZoC: Supply, **Vision** & Suppression"), just not wired to anything until this phase existed.
  - [ ] Units (Phase 5.4, now implemented) still don't feed this — `UnitInstance` has no `vision_radius` field (see its own doc comment: nothing reads one yet) and `FogOfWarManager._compute_visible_set()` only iterates `BuildingManager.get_all_buildings()`, not `UnitManager.get_all_units()`. `FogOfWarManager`'s vision-source contract (a hex-disk radius around a coord) is already generic enough that a unit will just be another source, not a special case, once both of those exist.
- [x] **2.6.3 Rendering Hooks**
  - [x] Strategic view (`HexCellView.set_fog_state()`): tints the whole tile via `Node2D.modulate` — black (hides biome color entirely) for `UNSEEN`, dimmed grey for `EXPLORED`, full color for `VISIBLE`. Shared with Tactical view via the new `FogVisuals.tint_color()` lookup (same pattern as `BuildingVisuals.category_color()`), so the two views agree.
  - [x] Tactical view (`TacticalHexView.set_fog_state()` / `LocalDetailManager`, Phase 2.5): `LocalDetailManager._hex_qualifies_for_detail()` now requires at least `EXPLORED` before hydrating a Tactical view at all — an `UNSEEN` hex zoomed into is still just blank/dark. Terrain/props/buildings stay visible (dimmed) at `EXPLORED` via the same modulate tint applied to the whole hydrated hex. The future mobile-entity layer (zombies, units — Phase 5) checking `VISIBLE` before drawing is deferred until that layer exists — nothing to gate yet.
- [x] **2.6.4 Night Integration (feeds Phase 5.1)** — `FogOfWarManager` connects to the new `TimeCycleManager.phase_changed` and calls `recompute()` on every Day↔Night flip.
  - [x] Vision radius shrinks at night except for lit sources (Gas Streetlamps, Watchtower searchlights), which hold or extend theirs. `_compute_visible_set()` shrinks every building's `vision_radius` by `NIGHT_VISION_PENALTY` (1, floored at 0) at night, except `BuildingDefinition.lit_at_night` sources (new field — Gas Streetlamp, Church Steeple Watchtower, and Phase 4.1's Searchlight Tower), which gain `NIGHT_LIT_BONUS` (1) instead — **decided:** extend, not just hold. Nothing about Phase 2.5-2.5.7/2.12's architecture needs to change to make room for this later: every `*Visuals.gd` lookup (`BuildingVisuals`, `TerrainVisuals`, `FogVisuals`, and whatever Phase 2.5.4/2.5.5 add for units/squads/zombies) already exists specifically so a future texture/sprite swap doesn't touch the logic that calls it — same pattern StrategicOverlayManager's own doc comment already states outright ("swappable for real icon art later without touching whatever triggers a marker").
- [x] **6.3.0 Terrain slice — SUPERSEDED by hand-authored SVG, done now (not later, not AI-generated).** The user asked to move toward a playable-looking state and picked hand-authored SVG (drawn directly, not sourced/AI-generated) over waiting for this phase's original plan, sequenced terrain before buildings/units. `assets/terrain/*.svg` — 11 files, one per `BiomeType`/`SoilFertility` combo (`urban`, `industrial`, `highland`, `waterway`, `wetland`, plus `farmland`/`moorland` × `lush`/`poor`/`desolate`), each a seamlessly-tiling hand-drawn pattern (soil tiers vary pattern/density as well as color — LUSH neat/dense, POOR sparse/patchy, DESOLATE cracked/dead — so the tiers still read by shape, not color alone). `TerrainVisuals.terrain_texture(biome, soil)` lazily loads/caches the matching `Texture2D` (same pattern as `BuildingCatalog`/`UnitCatalog`); an unauthored combo still falls back to the original flat `biome_color()` fill, so art can land incrementally with zero code changes elsewhere. `HexCellView` gained a `GroundMode` enum drawing the *same* texture resource two ways via `Polygon2D` uv/repeat params, no duplicate art or import config: Strategic uses explicit per-vertex uv (`HexCoord.corner_uvs()`) to paint the texture once as a map icon; Tactical (`TacticalHexView`) uses empty uv + `texture_repeat = ENABLED` to tile it as a naturalistic ground material across Phase 2.5.6's true-scale in-hex space. Verified via a temporary headless self-test (21/21 assertions: all 11 combos resolve to real textures, Strategic `Polygon2D` uses 6-point uv and doesn't tile, Tactical ground uses empty uv and does tile, an unauthored key still resolves to null not a crash) plus `check_gdscript.py` and a clean headless boot. **Buildings/units art (6.3.1/6.3.2 below) stays AI-generated/future as originally scoped** — this only covers the terrain slice, and only because hand-authored SVG turned out to be a fast, good-enough path for tileable ground textures specifically; it isn't a reversal of the AI-generated decision for the rest of this phase.

## 🧪 Phase 7: Narrative Campaign, Objectives & Steam Polish
> **Scope decided:** everything in this phase ships in the v1.0 Steam release **except mainland Europe** (7.7, a future paid expansion). The campaign is one continuous chain — Manchester → Trent Valley → London (open/organic, no gating) → Wales & Scotland (hard-gated, unlock together once the Final Mystery resolves) → Ireland (hard-gated, unlocks once Seafaring is researched, which itself needs Wales+Scotland retaken).
- [ ] **7.1 "The Southern Expedition" Campaign Arc (Manchester to London)**
  - [ ] **Act I: The Cottonopolis & The Pennine Barrier:** Secure Manchester (4 macro-hexes), clear the Chat Moss bogs, and establish defenses across the Peak District passes.
  - [ ] **Act II: The Trent Valley & Fenland Corridors:** Rebuild rail infrastructure through Birmingham, cross the River Trent, and defend supply routes against hordes emerging from the East Anglian Fens.
  - [ ] **Act III: The Thames Basin Citadel:** Breach the Chiltern Hills, secure outer London's 12 macro-hex perimeter, navigate the flooded London Underground, and reclaim Imperial headquarters.
  - [ ] Horde size/frequency (Phase 5.2's starting-small, ramping-up curve) should escalate roughly Act to Act — Manchester's small starting hordes are the tutorial-difficulty end of the same curve, not a separate easy-mode carve-out.
  - [ ] **Decided:** Acts I-III stay open/organic, same as the persistent map around them — nothing here is hard-locked like 7.4/7.5 below. They're narrative/objective waypoints over a map the player could technically wander anywhere on already; only the content beyond London gets an actual access lock.
- [ ] **7.2 Major Milestone Objectives System (`CampaignManager.gd`)**
  - [ ] **Primary Objective:** Establish a continuous, defended rail/road logistics link from Manchester to London.
  - [ ] **Secondary Objective:** Investigate regional relic sites (wrecked observatories, strange craters, underground testing facilities, captured UFO's etc.) revealing alien spore origins. Optional lore content — does not gate anything (see 7.2.1 below).
  - [ ] **Final Mystery:** Uncover the fate of Queen Victoria and the Imperial Cabinet inside the sealed Tower of London bunker. Did they know the alien invasion was coming this whole time? Leave it open ended for an expansion. **Decided:** this is the trigger for 7.4's Wales/Scotland unlock — a legitimate seat of government being re-established (Victoria found alive, or her fate and successor determined) is the in-fiction justification for authority extending past England. The Secondary Objective is explicitly *not* required — a player who skips the relic-site lore shouldn't be blocked from Wales/Scotland for it.
  - [ ] **7.2.1 Region Locking (extends Phase 2.6)** — not implemented, deliberately: no `CampaignManager` exists yet.
    - [ ] Wales, Scotland, and Ireland (plus all surrounding sea hexes) start `is_locked = true`, a `CampaignManager`-owned flag checked *before* Fog of War runs. A locked hex is forced `UNSEEN` regardless of any vision source and regardless of Phase 2.6's normal one-way `UNSEEN → EXPLORED` rule, and blocks all pathfinding through it for both player units and hordes — a full seal in both directions, not just a player-side restriction.
    - [ ] Wales and Scotland both flip `is_locked = false` together the moment the Final Mystery objective (above) resolves. Ireland (and its sea hexes) stays locked until Phase 2.9's Seafaring tech node is researched, which itself can't be researched until Wales *and* Scotland are both fully retaken (Phase 5.8 recapture state — actually held, not just unlocked-and-visible).
    - [ ] Unlocking is one-way and permanent — once open, a region behaves exactly like any other hex (normal fog, normal horde roaming, no special-casing).
- [ ] **7.3 Playtesting & Steam Integration**
  - [ ] Balance multi-week campaign progression from Manchester down to London, and onward through Wales/Scotland/Ireland.
  - [ ] Implement Steam Cloud Saves, Achievements, and Steam Deck controller layout support. Cloud Saves sync per-Campaign (Phase 2.8's named-campaign save model), not per-slot.
- [ ] **7.4 Wales & Scotland Reclamation** — plan only. **Decided:** two separate Acts (Act IV: Wales, Act V: Scotland), unlocked together by 7.2.1 but tackled in either order — both are deliberately more remote and resource-poor than England, leaning on a stable logistics tail from an already-developed home territory rather than any further scripted gate between them.
  - [ ] Each region gets its own geography (new `BritishGeographyData`-style seed content) and campaign-style milestones (7.2's pattern).
  - [ ] Reuses every system built through Phase 6 as-is — hex map, buildings, economy, ZoC, Discontent regions, tech tree — none of it is campaign-specific, which is what makes this cheap to add on top rather than a second game.
- [ ] **7.5 Naval Logistics & Seafaring (Ireland Unlock)** — plan only, and the biggest net-new system in this list, but confirmed **in scope for v1.0** (not deferred). Ireland was part of the UK in 1890 and is only reachable by sea, so reclaiming it needs a genuinely new transport layer: ports (a new building, and the payoff of Phase 2.9's Seafaring tech node), ships (a new unit-like entity with its own pathfinding over open-water hexes rather than land — see 5.5), and sea supply lines as a `LogisticsNetwork` segment type alongside road/rail/canal. Locked (7.2.1) until Wales and Scotland are both retaken.
  - [ ] Act VI: Ireland — same escalating-difficulty/milestone pattern as 7.1/7.4, the far end of the campaign's difficulty curve.
- [ ] **7.6 Difficulty, Win/Loss & New Game Setup** — plan only.
  - [ ] **Decided: defeat is an economic/capability elimination check, not a territorial one.** The game only ends when the player has (a) not enough stockpiled resources across every `ResourceManager` type to create new units and retake lost land and (b) no total daily production from anything still standing, and or (c) no remaining building capable of recruiting a unit or expanding onto a new hex. Holding even a single settlement with the ability to recruit new units to expand means they still have a chance of winning.
  - [ ] Recovering from near-total loss is meant to be *possible in principle but hard in practice*: Phase 5.8 (Territory Capture & Loss) is what lets a lost district be recaptured at all, and Phase 5.9 (Casualty Conversion) is what makes recapturing it genuinely difficult — a densely populated settlement that falls converts its own people into the zombies now defending it, so the hardest place to retake is exactly the one most worth retaking.
  - [ ] Difficulty presets (horde aggression/frequency, upkeep drain rates) and starting-map/seed choice at New Game. New Game means naming a Campaign (Phase 2.8) — this is the tuning knob on top of Phase 5.2's baseline starting-small/ramping-up curve, not a separate system.
- [ ] **7.7 Future Expansion Hook (Continental Europe)** — plan only, no build work now, and the one deliberate v1.0 exclusion (see phase header). Confirms the architecture already supports this without rework: axial hex coordinates (`HexCoord`) are unbounded, `BritishGeographyData.MAP_BOUNDS` is a single adjustable constant rather than anything hard-coded deeper into the generator, and named geography is entirely data (`GeographyFeature` seed lists) — a future Europe expansion is "author a new seed data file and raise the bounds," not a rewrite. Naval logistics (7.5) is the actual prerequisite, since Europe is inherently sea-separated from Great Britain. Framed as a future paid expansion (~£5) if the base game finds an audience, not free post-launch content.
