## 🔧 Technical Summary
Godot 4.x/GDScript RTS built on a continuous axial hex map of real UK+Ireland geography (Natural Earth's public-domain coastline data, recalibrated to a genuine ~25 sq mi/hex scale — 154×179 bounds, ~4,700 land hexes of ~27,566 total). A two-tier camera (Strategic hex-icon view / Tactical true-scale in-hex view, `HexCoord.HEX_SIZE` = 512 world units per hex) with four LOD bands drives everything from country-scale strategy down to individual soldiers and props. Full economy (8 resource types, 21 building types across Housing/Industry/Agriculture/Defense, a 6-node tech tree), an 18-unit roster (6 tiers × 3 roles) with real combat, morale/veterancy, and per-Special-unit mechanical identities, wandering zombie hordes with noise/light-attraction AI and wall-siege behavior, a full wall/gate/ZoC/supply-line layer, Fog of War, Day/Night cycles, save/load, and a complete code-drawn HUD (build menu, unit orders, minimap, tech tree, alerts). **Phases 1 → 2.12, 4, 5 (core), 6, and 6.3's art-pipeline infrastructure are built and playable end to end.** Not built: the sewer/underground layer (Phase 3), late-game reclamation and the entire narrative campaign (Phase 5.11, Phase 7), and the 18 AI-generated unit PNGs (infrastructure ready, images not yet generated — no image-generation tool has been available in this environment). A real, documented architecture debt exists around hex-grid granularity — see that dedicated section below before starting any biome/wall/logistics work.

## 📖 The Setting
January 1890. Something came down over the Pennines in the night, and within a week the dead of Manchester were walking. The city sealed itself hard — gaslit checkpoints, a militia raised from constables and navvies, whatever the Industrial Revolution could throw up in a fortnight — and held. Beyond the wire, the rest of Britain went dark: no telegraph, no trains, no light after sundown, just the slow drift of the hungry across a country that used to be the workshop of the world. You inherit that quarantine: one lit town in an island of soot and silence, armed with nothing but cast iron, coal smoke, and Victorian grit — no borrowed future technology, just the Industrial Revolution pushed as far as it will go. South of you, Queen Victoria and the Imperial Cabinet are sealed in a bunker under the Tower of London, and nobody above ground knows if they're still alive. Scattered through the wreckage are stranger traces still — wrecked observatories, craters that don't match any war, rumours of things that didn't fall from the sky by accident. Your job isn't rescue. It's to hold the light, push roads and rail south hex by hex, and find out — expedition by expedition — whether Manchester's quarantine was the last sane decision the old world made, or the first sign that whatever did this was already here.

## 🏛️ Project Architecture & Design Overview

- **Core Loop:** Continuous persistent expansion across Victorian Britain hex sectors. Build defenses, automate patrols, manage daily resource upkeeps (Food/Energy/Gunpowder), and protect supply lines while working in the background.
- **Aesthetic Direction:** Grounded late-18th/19th-century Industrial Revolution (brick, cast iron, coal smoke, authentic Victorian architecture)—explicitly **no** retro-futuristic steampunk tropes. Applies even to the top-tier "mechanized" units in Phase 5.4 — heavy steam engineering (armoured traction engines, rail guns, breaching cranes), never a humanoid battle-mech.
- **Dual Perspective Engine:** Dynamic camera toggle between **Top-Down Orthographic** (tactical layout) and **2.5D Isometric** (cinematic viewing).
- **Multi-Layer Macro-Hex System:** Continuous tilemap hiding an underlying axial hex grid `(q, r)`. Each hex represents a 5x5 mile macro-region (~25 sq miles). Cities scale dynamically across multiple macro-tiles—Manchester spans 4 hexes (~100 sq miles), while Greater London spans 12 hexes (~300 sq miles). Features internal district partitioning and subterranean layers (Sewers/London Underground).
- **Time Controls & Day/Night Cycle:** Global `TickManager` supporting `0x` (Pause), `1x`, `2x`, `3x` and `5x` speeds (`Engine.time_scale`). Features a 40-minute real-time day/night cycle (20 minutes Day / 20 minutes Night at 1x speed).
- **Accessibility:** Every color-coded piece of state (biome, soil, Zone of Control, fog-of-war, building category, alerts) is paired with a distinct shape/icon, never color alone — cheap to hold to now, expensive to retrofit once more systems lean on color.
- **World State Framing:** Outside player-held ground, 1890 Britain is functionally dead — dark, unlit, and roamed by hordes with no fixed home (Phase 5.10). Player settlements are the only lit, defended points on the map (Phase 2.6's Fog of War and Phase 5.10's horde roaming are two views of the same idea — a beacon surrounded by darkness). The whole game arc runs from purely defensive "hold the light" play early-to-mid game toward proactively reclaiming and re-lighting the wider country late-game (Phase 5.11, Phase 7.4, Phase 7.5).

## 🧩 Known Architecture Constraint: Hex-Grid Over-Coupling
> User feedback, not yet actioned: too much of this game's state is keyed directly to a single `HexCell`/`Vector2i` coordinate when it should be more granular — **biomes and walls named explicitly**, but the pattern is broader than either alone. Documented here so a future pass has a real, evidenced starting point instead of relitigating from scratch. Nothing below has been changed because of this entry — it's a caveat on the "Multi-Layer Macro-Hex System" bullet in the Architecture Overview above, not a task list yet.

**Why this is a real constraint, not just a style nitpick:** one hex is ~25 sq. miles (`HexCell`'s own doc comment). Every field below is a SINGLE scalar value for that entire area — real terrain doesn't split cleanly into one uniform biome/soil/feature per 25-square-mile block, and it shows most at Tactical zoom (Phase 2.5), where the camera is close enough to see individual soldiers standing on ground that is flatly, uniformly one texture with zero internal variation.

**What's hex-locked today, concretely:**
- **Biomes:** `HexCell.biome_type`, `soil_fertility`, `terrain_feature`, `elevation` — all one value per hex, gating placement (`allowed_biomes`/`allowed_soil_fertility` checks) and passability (`is_passable()`) for the whole area at once. A hex is entirely Farmland or entirely Wetland; there's no concept of "a stream cuts through the corner of this hex" or "the near edge is drained, the far edge is still bog."
- **Walls:** `WallSegment.hex_a`/`hex_b` — literally can only run along one of a hex's 6 geometric edges (`WallManager.get_placement_error()` hard-rejects anything where the two hexes aren't adjacent — confirmed this directly this session). No angle, no partial-edge run, no wall that cuts across a hex's interior.
- **Supply lines:** `SupplyLineSegment.hex_a`/`hex_b` — the exact same shape as walls (`WallManager`'s own doc comment even says it "mirrors LogisticsNetwork's... chain of individually-tracked segments between hex pairs"). A road/rail/canal is either "the whole edge" or doesn't exist.
- **Zone of Control, Noise, Fog of War:** `LogisticsNetwork`, `NoiseManager`, `FogOfWarManager` all store/derive their coverage as a flat per-hex value (`Dictionary` keyed `Vector2i -> state`), radiating via `HexCoord.hex_disk()`. A hex is either VISIBLE or it isn't; either covered by ZoC or it isn't; either under noise pressure or it isn't — no gradient across the hex's own area.
- **Districts (`District.gd`/`DistrictPartitioner.gd`) are the one nominally "sub-hex" system that exists, and it doesn't actually solve this.** It's a fixed categorical template — a settlement hex always gets exactly "Urban Center + Industrial Estate (safe) + Uncleared Wilderness (contested)," a non-settlement hex always gets exactly one "fully contested wilderness" — with no position, shape, or real geography behind any of the three. It answers "is combat active somewhere in this hex," not "where." Worth knowing about before designing a real fix: this is the closest existing prior art, but it's a label, not geometry.

**What's already NOT hex-locked, for contrast — this isn't a from-scratch problem:** buildings and units already carry a real sub-hex `local_position` offset (Phase 2.5.6/2.5.7's "true-scale in-hex coordinates," `HexCoord.HEX_SIZE` = 512 world units per hex) — used directly this session to place the free starting Cast Iron Foundry visibly apart from the Town Hall on the same hex. The *placement/presentation* layer already proved out real sub-hex positioning years of phases ago; it's specifically the *terrain/biome/wall/logistics data* layer that never followed suit. A future fix has a working precedent to extend from, not a blank page.

- [ ] Revisit if/when a dedicated sub-hex spatial partition gets designed — Districts' `Array[District]` per hex is the closest existing seam to extend (real position/shape data, not just a type+contested flag), but that's a real design decision for that future pass, not decided here.

## 📐 Standing Directive: Design Against *They Are Billions*, Don't Invent New Mechanics
> User feedback, 2026-08-09, verbatim in spirit: "This is all built in They Are Billions but for some reason I'm having to spec it out again... you aren't basing these mechanics on what I am [referencing] and instead inventing your own mechanics." This project's own pitch (Technical Summary, Phase 2.5's own header) explicitly names *They Are Billions* as the reference for the country-scale-to-individual-soldier feel — that reference has NOT consistently been used as an actual mechanical source when building wall, siege, and movement systems specifically. **Before designing or redesigning wall placement/destruction, zombie movement/AI, or siege behavior, research how *They Are Billions* actually implements it first, and match that model** rather than deriving a new one from first principles. The two concrete cases logged below (wall segmentation, unit/zombie movement) are the first identified instances — treat this as a standing constraint on any future work touching either system, not a one-time fix.

## 🐞 Open Player-Reported Bugs (logged 2026-08-09) — Not Yet Fixed
> Reported directly by the user after playtesting the build produced by commits `82da243` and `e651877` (both same-day, both explicitly claiming to fix building/wall visibility). None of these are fixed yet — this section only records and roots them, per the user's explicit "log these bugs" request. Do not mark any item below done without an actual post-fix screenshot re-check (see the first bug for why headless-only verification isn't sufficient here).

- [ ] **Buildings are still never visible on the map, at all — reported twice in the same message for emphasis.** This is the THIRD report of this exact symptom: `82da243` ("I can't see the walls on the maps, nor any buildings at all") and `e651877` ("I still can't see any buildings I place") both already claimed to fix it this same day, and it is still broken. Both prior fixes were verified via a headless render-tree dump / headless diagnostic (`e651877`'s own commit message) rather than an actual rendered screenshot taken *after* the fix — that's the likely process failure, not necessarily the code. **Before claiming this fixed again: launch the real windowed build, place a building, and visually confirm it in a screenshot** — the project's own reusable screenshot recipe (`Start-Process` + `EnumWindows`/`SetForegroundWindow` ALT-keypress workaround + `CopyFromScreen`, see project memory) already exists for exactly this, it just hasn't actually been run post-fix.
- [ ] **Garrisons cannot be placed anywhere, ever.** Root cause hypothesis, confirmed in code but not yet reproduced live: `BuildingCatalog._garrison()` sets `allowed_biomes = [GameEnums.BiomeType.URBAN]` and `requires_settlement = true` (`scripts/buildings/data/BuildingCatalog.gd:152-153`). `is_settlement = true` + `biome_type = URBAN` are set in exactly one place in the whole codebase — `HexMapGenerator._apply_feature()`'s `SETTLEMENT` feature branch (`scripts/world/generation/HexMapGenerator.gd:110-113`), run once at map-gen time for the handful of hand-authored named cities (Manchester, Birmingham, London, etc.). **There is no player-driven way to found a new settlement or urbanize a hex** — so a Garrison can only ever legally be built on one of those few pre-authored city hexes, and will unconditionally reject everywhere else. Needs a live repro to confirm whether it *also* fails directly on Manchester's own settlement hex (a second, separate placement bug) or only away from one (in which case the real bug is the total absence of any player-facing "why" — `get_placement_error()` does return a reason string today, so check whether `BuildPlacementController`'s ghost/rejection path is actually surfacing it for this specific building type).
- [ ] **Wall segments are hex-edge-locked (one segment = one full hex edge, ~5 real miles at this project's own ~25 sq mi/hex scale) and destroy as one monolithic piece.** Confirmed architecturally, not a guess: `WallSegment.hex_a`/`hex_b` (`scripts/defense/data/WallSegment.gd:20-21`) — the class's own doc comment states placement is "pixel-precise snapping... a placement-UI concern for a future... flow, not this data class's job — it only needs to know which two hexes' shared edge it defends," i.e. freeform sub-hex geometry was explicitly deferred, never built. `WallManager.get_placement_error()` hard-rejects any two hexes that aren't adjacent. This is the same limitation already named in this doc's own "Known Architecture Constraint" section above (walls bullet) — but that entry only flagged it as a someday-revisit; **the user has now given a concrete spec, matching *They Are Billions*': freehand click-drag placement, and a hard maximum of 100 real-world meters per segment — anything longer auto-splits into multiple independently-HP'd, independently-destructible segments**, so a horde breaching one 100m stretch never wipes an entire multi-mile wall line in one hit. This requires the sub-hex wall-geometry rework the Architecture Constraint section already anticipated, not a tuning number change.
- [ ] **No way to select a placed wall or building to repair it — the repair functions are unreachable dead code from the UI today.** Confirmed by grep across `scripts/ui/` and `scripts/world/`: `BuildingManager.repair_building()`/`WallManager.repair_segment()` both exist and are individually correct, but have ZERO call sites anywhere outside their own defining files — no button, menu entry, or click handler ever calls either. `UnitCommandController._select_at()` (`scripts/world/UnitCommandController.gd:135-147`) only ever emits `building_selected(coord)` — a raw hex coordinate, not a specific `BuildingInstance` (breaks down the moment two buildings share a hex via their real sub-hex `local_position`, e.g. the starting Town Hall + Cast Iron Foundry) — and `UnitPanelView._on_building_selected()` (`scripts/ui/UnitPanelView.gd:51-52`) uses that coordinate ONLY to render a unit-training panel ("Train at %s"), never a repair option. **Walls have no selection path at all** — `_select_at()` has no wall-hit-test branch whatsoever today; a wall segment cannot be clicked or selected under any circumstance.
- [ ] **Map terrain props with a canonical "right way up" (trees, etc.) spawn at fully random rotation, so many render upside-down.** Confirmed: `LocalDetailGenerator.gd:51` — `prop.rotation = rng.randf_range(0.0, TAU)` — applied uniformly to every prop type with no per-type distinction between rotation-agnostic props (rubble, rocks) and orientation-sensitive ones (trees, lampposts, fences). Needs a per-`PropInstance`-type flag (e.g. `allow_free_rotation`) gating whether `TAU`-range randomization applies at all, vs. a small ±jitter (or zero) for anything with a real "up."
- [ ] **Zombies and units don't move organically — they beeline dead-straight to a hex's exact center, then pivot, in a visibly programmatic way, instead of wandering like something alive reacting to stimuli.** Confirmed in code: `MovementStepper.advance_toward_hex()` (`scripts/world/MovementStepper.gd:75-96`) always aims the entity in a perfectly straight line at `target_hex`'s EXACT center (`HexCoord.axial_to_world(target_hex)`) for every single hex-to-hex leg of a path — no heading jitter, no curved or wandering motion within a hex, nothing stimulus-driven about the path itself (only *which hex is chosen next* has any bias, via `HordeManager`'s existing `WANDERING`/`ATTRACTED` state machine — the leg-by-leg motion underneath is always ruler-straight regardless). `TacticalEntityLayer`'s squad-figure formation (`scripts/world/TacticalEntityLayer.gd:358-359`, `angle := TAU * float(index) / float(total)`) then places a unit's 5 figures at perfectly even angles around that dead-straight-moving point, which is very likely the literal "rotate around it in a programmatic way" the user is seeing. **User's explicit ask, matching the standing directive above: research how *They Are Billions* actually drives zombie movement (organic drift, genuinely reacting to noise/light stimuli rather than a rigid hex-snap state machine) and match that model** rather than continuing to extend this project's own from-scratch approach.
- [ ] **Buildings aren't sized against the real ~5mi×5mi hex scale.** `TacticalHexView.BUILDING_HALF_SIZE = 70.0` (`scripts/world/TacticalHexView.gd:274`) is a flat placeholder world-unit constant, chosen purely for "does it read as a building against the terrain at a glance" (`e651877`'s own commit message) — never derived from `HexCoord.HEX_SIZE` (512 world units = one full ~5-mile hex edge-to-edge) or any real building footprint. Needs a real derivation: pick a plausible real-world building footprint (tens of meters, not miles) and express `BUILDING_HALF_SIZE` as that fraction of `HEX_SIZE`'s known real-world span, so a building reads as a correctly-tiny structure inside a true-to-scale 5-mile hex rather than an arbitrarily chosen box.

## 📝 Phase 1: Engine Foundation, Terrain & Geography — ✅ Built
Godot 4.x project scaffolded (`/scripts`, `/scenes`, `/assets`), background-execution-friendly display settings, and `CameraController.gd`'s dual TOP_DOWN/ISOMETRIC toggle (a "faked" 2D rotate-45°-and-squash-Y trick over the flat world, not true isometric art — see the Architecture Constraint section above for how this interacts with unit art).

**Hex map & real geography (`HexGridMap.gd`, `BritishGeographyData.gd`):** the whole UK+Ireland landmass, baked from Natural Earth's public-domain 10m coastline GeoJSON (not hand-authored), recalibrated to a genuine ~25 sq mi/hex scale — `MAP_BOUNDS` is 154×179 (27,566 hexes), ~4,692 land. `GameEnums.BiomeType.OCEAN` is impassable and skips `HexCellView` spawn entirely for performance (~1.6s full headless boot). Named settlements/features (Manchester, Birmingham, London, Pennines, Chilterns, Thames/Mersey/Trent, Chat Moss, the Fens, etc.) project from real lon/lat via an equirectangular pipeline, snapped to nearest land hex where needed. Wales/Scotland/Ireland have landmass but no authored content yet (locked/inaccessible, Phase 7.2.1/7.4/7.5). `CoastlineOutlineView.gd` draws the land/ocean boundary even through unexplored Fog of War — public geography, not a gameplay secret. **Known, disclosed risk:** the full map span (~214,600×136,700 world units) is past the ~100,000-unit range where single-precision float jitter typically starts (`HexCoord.HEX_SIZE`'s own doc comment) — a floating-origin camera rebase would be the real fix, not attempted; live screenshot testing has shown no visible jitter so far.

Granular soil fertility (`HexCell.soil_fertility`) and District partitioning (`District.gd`/`DistrictPartitioner.gd` — Urban Center/Industrial Estate/Uncleared Wilderness per settlement hex) both implemented; see the Architecture Constraint section above for the real limits of both.

## 🏭 Phase 2: Historical Building Tree, Economy & Logistics — ✅ Built
**Building tree (`BuildingCatalog.gd`/`BuildingManager.gd`):** 21 building types across Housing & Civil (Terraced Tenement, Workhouse, Church Steeple Watchtower, Gas Streetlamp, Telegraph Relay Office, Steam Printing Press, Town Hall, Garrison), Industry & Extraction (Timber Camp, Clay Brickworks, Charcoal Kiln, Coal Pithead, Cast Iron Foundry, Saltpetre & Powder Mill, Forward Ammo Dump), Agriculture (Tenant Farm, Grain Silo, Cattle Yard), and Defense Works (Searchlight Tower, Ditch, Oil Pit — the latter two cost-data-only, placed via `WallManager`).

**Resource economy (`ResourceManager.gd`):** 8 resource types (Food, Energy, Gunpowder, Research Points, Wood, Bricks, Cast Iron, Reinforced Concrete — the last with no producer yet, a disclosed gap). Food and Gunpowder are real daily drains; **Energy is deliberately NOT a daily drain** — a one-time grid-capacity allocation spent/granted once at construction/repair and refunded/withdrawn on ruin (`_apply_construction_energy()`/`_refund_construction_energy()`), explicitly excluded from the daily tally. A ruined producer's withdrawal is allowed to drive Energy negative (`ResourceManager.remove()`, unconditional unlike `spend()`) — an honest deficit, not silently floored.

**Zone of Control & supply (`LogisticsNetwork.gd`):** Military ZoC (Forward Ammo Dumps, Garrisons, Watchtowers, combat units) projects a real hex-disk aura (`MILITARY_AURA_RADIUS`, not single-hex), doubling as a vision source and a Tactical-hydration qualifier. Civilian ZoC (Town Halls, Churches, Telegraph Relays) requires secure ground, covers the whole hex, unlocks walls/recruitment. Both visualized (`ZoneOfControlVisuals.gd` — outline vs. translucent fill, real shape distinction). Severing a supply line (road/rail/canal, `SupplyLineSegment`) deactivates the ZoC it was carrying.

## 🔎 Phase 2.5: Tactical Zoom & Local Detail Layer — ✅ Built
Added mid-development to guarantee a *They Are Billions*-at-country-scale feel — zoom seamlessly from a large chunk of Great Britain down to individual soldiers. A hard zoom-threshold (`CameraController.tactical_zoom_threshold`) switches between the abstract Strategic hex view and a continuous, coordinate-precise Tactical view of one hex's real ~25-sq-mi footprint — pure camera zoom, no loading screen. Tactical detail only hydrates for settled/frontier/ZoC-covered hexes (`LocalDetailManager`), following the camera as a contiguous neighborhood.

**Scale (`HexCoord.HEX_SIZE` = 512, was 64):** a straight 8x world-space multiplier gives Tactical zoom a genuinely large in-hex space to pan across — every building/unit/horde position already routes through `axial_to_world()`, so this stayed a single-constant change. `max_zoom` raised twice since (0.3125 → 6.0 → 12.0) for individual-figure legibility and user-requested extra zoom headroom.

**Entities at Tactical zoom (`TacticalEntityLayer.gd`):** rendering splits by tier, not role — Tier 0-3 units draw as a ring of 5 (`SQUAD_SIZE`) code-drawn figures around `local_position`, Tier 4-5 draw one larger model. Every `Horde` draws a capped figure cluster (`MAX_RENDERED_ZOMBIES` = 30, a placeholder well short of the eventual GPU-batched ~5,000-100,000 target). Squad headcount is derived (`ceil(current_hp / (max_hp/SQUAD_SIZE))`), not stored — a fallen squad member becomes a real casualty zombie via the same figures-lost pipeline `CombatCoordinator` uses. Garrison-gated healing (5%/tick while on ZoC-covered ground) is the project's first regen mechanic.

**Four-tier LOD (`GameEnums.TacticalFidelity`):** Strategic icons are untouched by fidelity entirely. Tactical splits into LOW (uniform blob per category — units/hordes only, never per-type — shape-and-color differentiated, buildings never simplified), MEDIUM (role/tier-shaped unit markers, fixed-size zombie cluster), and HIGH (full individual figures, today's real props/buildings). Buildings/props render identically at Medium/High — no real-art distinction ever existed to justify a difference there.

Building placement got exact `local_position` precision (`place_building_at_world()`); buildings AND wall segments can only be placed while zoomed into Tactical view (unit training stays reachable from both). Placement rejection plays the project's first-ever audio (`AlertTones.negative_tone()`, synthesized, no asset file).

## 🌫️ Phase 2.6: Fog of War — ✅ Built (one disclosed gap)
Three-state `GameEnums.FogState` (`FogOfWarManager.gd`): `UNSEEN` → `EXPLORED` (one-way, forever) ↔ `VISIBLE` (toggles freely). Losing a vision source doesn't drop a hex to `EXPLORED` instantly — a 3s grace period first. Vision sources: every building's own `vision_radius` (default 0 = own hex; Gas Streetlamp 1, Watchtower 2) plus Military/Civilian ZoC coverage (doubling as vision, the original Phase 2.3 intent). Strategic view tints via `modulate`; Tactical requires at least `EXPLORED` to hydrate at all. Night halves vision radius except `lit_at_night` sources (Gas Streetlamp, Church Watchtower, Searchlight Tower), which *extend* theirs instead.

- [ ] **2.6.2:** Units still don't feed Fog of War — `UnitInstance` has no `vision_radius` field yet; `FogOfWarManager` only iterates buildings. The vision-source contract is already generic enough that a unit is just another source once both exist.
- [ ] **2.6.5 Region Lock Override (feeds Phase 7.2):** not implemented — no `CampaignManager` exists yet to check an `is_locked` flag before visibility computes.

## 💾 Phase 2.8: Persistence & Save/Load — ✅ Built
`/scripts/persistence` (`SaveLoadManager.gd`). Terrain never needs saving (fixed noise seeds, byte-identical regeneration); what does: building instances (`BuildingSaveEntry`), resource stockpile+caps, supply-line segments/severed state, `TickManager`'s day/speed, and Fog of War's explored/visible state (genuinely earned, can't be re-derived — restored last, overriding any transient recompute). ## 🍞 Phase 2.10: Population Sustenance & Starvation — ✅ Built
Folded into `BuildingManager._on_day_completed()`. `BuildingInstance.current_population` is real mutable per-instance state (seeded from `population_provided`, not re-derived from it). Daily food ratio = available/(demand); `ratio >= 1.0` no penalty, `0.5–1.0` scales production down to match the ratio directly (75% fed → 75% output), `< 0.5` triggers real starvation deaths scaled by severity. Military units are exempt by construction — they're never fed into the population/Food math at all. Every starved civilian becomes a casualty zombie at its own hex (`civilians_starved` → `HordeManager.add_casualty_zombies()`). A genuine surplus (`ratio >= 1.5`) grants a small capped production bonus (+10% max) and gradual population regrowth (+1/day/building).

## 🧱 Phase 4: Defensive Construction, Infrastructure & Chokepoints — ✅ Built
`/scripts/defense` (`WallCatalog.gd`/`WallSegment.gd`/`WallManager.gd`, mirroring `BuildingCatalog`'s split and `LogisticsNetwork`'s hex-edge-segment shape). Walls are a chain of individually-placed, individually-HP'd segments between adjacent hexes (`hex_a`/`hex_b`) — Wooden → Brick → Concrete, tech-gated, each segment upgradable for 50% of a fresh build. **Gates** (`WallSegment.is_gate`, added this session) are the same tier/cost/segment at 40% HP (`WallCatalog.GATE_HP_FRACTION`) — a deliberate weak point, not an undefended gap; walls only ever block *hordes*, never the player's own units/logistics.

Hordes genuinely siege whichever specific segment their drift path reaches (`HordeManager._siege_wall()`), with a 2x damage bonus against walls; Ditches/Oil Pits inflict real counter-damage. `WallManager.repair_segment()` restores a breached segment at 50% of a fresh build. **Outer vs. legacy-inner walls** is a purely positional classification derived live from ZoC coverage (`WallManager.is_legacy_segment()`, both hexes covered) rather than a stored field or second combat system — the fallback-bulkhead behavior itself needed zero new code, since a horde's per-edge peek already re-checks every boundary it crosses, not just the first. Searchlight Tower + `CombatEngine`'s `incoming_damage_multiplier` give garrisoned units a real defense bonus, doubled at night near a lit tower.

`ReclamationManager.gd`: repairs severed supply lines (a costed action wrapping `LogisticsNetwork`'s existing instant toggle) and drains swamps (marsh/peat bog → passable Moorland, Desolate→Poor soil — the one case where terrain state needs real saving, since it no longer regenerates byte-identically from the seed).

**This session's economy-balance pass added:** a free starting Cast Iron Foundry + Tenant Farm seeded alongside the Town Hall (`BuildingManager.seed_starting_buildings()`), connected by a real `HexPathfinder`-routed corridor and enclosed in a free starting wall perimeter with gates (`WallManager.seed_starting_defenses()`) — see git history/session notes for the full mechanism; not written up as its own phase since it's tuning on top of already-built systems, not new architecture.

## ⚔️ Phase 5: Threat Mechanics, Day/Night & Horde Intel — ✅ Built (core), Phase 5.11 not started
The single largest system in the project. `TimeCycleManager.gd` (autoload after `TickManager`) derives Day/Night from `TickManager.get_day_progress()`.

- **5.1 Day/Night:** Zombies move at 0.65x by day, 1.5x by night (exact design-doc number). Units move 1.2x faster and hit 1.1x harder by day. Fog contracts at night except lit sources. Victorian calendar date (`get_calendar_date_string()`, campaign day 1 = 1 Jan 1890).
  - [ ] Construction/gather +20% by day: blocked on a sub-day production tick (currently once/full-day only).
  - [ ] Aggression +100% by night: no aggression stat exists on hordes yet.
  - [ ] Sewer eruption 2x by night: blocked on Phase 3.
- **5.2 Horde spawn & industrial attraction:** `NoiseManager.gd` — per-hex noise Dictionary from `BuildingDefinition.noise_output` (Industry & Extraction buildings only), flat-radius aura (no falloff), overlapping sources sum, doubled at night, lit-at-night sources add extra attraction after dark. World seeds with 3 small (10-25) roaming hordes on frontier hexes (fixed seed 1892), plus a small daily chance (0.35) of a tiny 1-3 ambient spawn — a "lone zombie" is just a small `Horde`, not a separate entity type.
- **5.3 Reconnaissance:** `HordeManager.get_eta_seconds()` — real ETA from continuous movement math, not a hex-count estimate, surfaced in the HUD for the soonest-arriving currently-visible `ATTRACTED` horde. "Observed range" reuses Fog of War's existing `VISIBLE` state rather than a bespoke observation-post mechanic.
- **5.4 Unit roster & combat (`CombatEngine.gd`):** 18 units, 6 tiers × 3 roles, one shared balancing curve (`UnitCatalog._unit()`). Tier 0-3 are individual soldiers, Tier 4-5 are heavy grounded engineering (never battle-mechs). **Current roster (renamed/rebalanced this session — see below):** Truncheoneer/Toxophilite/Outrider (T0), Navvy/Yeoman Marksman/Grenadier (T1), Redcoat/Rifleman/Chasseur (T2), Highlander/Sharpshooter/Dragoon (T3), Traction Ram/Maxim Quadricycle/Searchlight Tender (T4), Holt Breaker/Field Howitzer Gun Tractor/Armoured Command Car (T5). Ranged units (Yeoman Marksman+) suffer a fragile forced-melee penalty at zero Gunpowder; Toxophilite is exempt (arrows aren't tracked). Special-role abilities (`GameEnums.UnitAbility`) are no longer Special-exclusive after this session's redesign: **Traction Ram + Holt Breaker** (both Melee) share `TRAMPLE_KNOCKBACK` — a tier 4→5 "crush the horde by weight" pair with no stun, heavier HP, slower speed; **Searchlight Tender** carries `MOBILE_SUPPLY_DUMP` (a moving ZoC aura that doubles as vision — matches its "illuminate/coordinate" description literally, since ZoC coverage already doubles as vision); **Armoured Command Car** carries the new `RAPID_RESPONSE` (fast, lightly armed, no bespoke effect code, pure stat lean); **Outrider** (`UNARMED_SCOUT`, 0 damage/1.6x speed) and **Chasseur** (`MOUNTED_FIREARM`, 1.3x damage/1.4x speed) and **Grenadier** (`EXPLOSIVE_SPLASH`, 1.5x damage) and **Dragoon** (`CHARGE_KNOCKBACK`, real 1-hex knockback + 1s stun) unchanged. **Maxim Quadricycle**/**Field Howitzer Gun Tractor** (Ranged, no ability) are now differentiated purely by stats — fast/fragile/rapid-fire vs. slow/fragile/devastating.
  - [ ] Per-unit tech upgrade nodes (beyond the whole-tier unlock) stay unbuilt — no balancing pass exists for what one would even do.
- **5.5 Pathfinding (`HexPathfinder.gd`):** stateless A* over the hex graph, terrain-cost-weighted, logistics-line-discounted. **Continuous local movement (`MovementStepper.gd`):** replaced the old once-per-20-real-seconds hex-snap with genuine real-time world-coordinate motion, threading multiple hex boundaries per frame at high tick speeds; units/zombies steer around scattered props/buildings (`ObstacleRadii.gd`, Reynolds-style nudge, not guaranteed to escape a dense cluster). `unit_moved`/`horde_moved` still fire once per hex boundary crossed, preserving every existing signal contract.
- **5.6 Orders (`UnitOrderController.gd`):** Move/Attack-Move/Hold/Patrol/Garrison/Retrain. Combat resolution doesn't care what order a unit is under — contact triggers a fight regardless (`CombatCoordinator`). Retrain converts an existing unit to a newly-unlocked tier for 50% cost, instant, anywhere, carrying over *relative* HP.
- **5.7 Morale & Veterancy (`UnitMorale.gd`):** stateless, equal-weighted average of HP fraction / Gunpowder-supplied / rank (Rookie→Veteran→Elite from kill count) / tier. Low morale caps damage at 0.6x floor; veterancy adds +10%/+25% flat. A "kill" = destroying a `Horde` outright (hordes are a population, not discrete targets).
  - [ ] A famine-severity fifth input (units undersupplied but not dying) is a reasonable extension, deliberately not wired — would need a live `BuildingManager` reference this class is meant to stay free of.
- **5.8 Territory (`TerritoryController.gd`):** a district flips contested when a building on it ruins while a horde occupies the hex (not merely passing through); flips back once the player clears the horde, no rebuild requirement. A still-ruined building correctly stays excluded from ZoC even once its hex is safe again.
  - [ ] Ruin *repair* on recapture isn't built yet — recapturing un-contests the district but the specific ruined building stays rubble until Phase 5.12's repair action is used.
- **5.9 Casualty conversion (extends `HordeManager`):** every lost civilian and military unit becomes a zombie at the exact hex it died — starvation, unit death, and even a single lost squad figure (sub-unit granularity) all feed `add_casualty_zombies()`.
  - [ ] Whole-building-population-at-once conversion at ruin scale is Phase 5.12's job, already partly real (see below) but not yet framed as "a formerly dense district becomes one of the biggest threats on the map" at full scale.
- **5.10 Horde roaming (`HordeManager.gd`):** `WANDERING` (biased drift via `HexPathfinder`, no beeline), `ATTRACTED` (noise/light-drawn via `NoiseManager`, locally scoped — a horde only scans within its own awareness radius, not the whole map), and wall-siege `ATTACKING`. Contact triggers combat regardless of state. Hordes periodically merge (co-located, 0.1/tick chance) or split (size ≥20, 0.05/tick chance). Only hordes ≥100 get a Strategic-map marker; smaller ones are Tactical-only.
  - [ ] Performance-at-scale (cheap abstracted simulation far from the player, full AI only near it; GPU-batched rendering for true horde-scale figure counts) is a documented future technique, not implemented.
- [ ] **5.11 Countryside Reclamation (Late-Game Offense)** — plan only, not started. Mid/late-game shift from purely defensive play to actively hunting and thinning ambient horde population; the mechanic underneath Phase 7's campaign corridor-clearing. A cleared hex isn't permanently safe — ambient spawning continues.
- **5.12 Building ruins (extends `BuildingInstance`):** a destroyed building becomes a persistent rubble state (`is_ruined`, stops producing/consuming/housing) rather than disappearing, rendering as a distinct silhouette. Its housed population converts to zombies on the spot. Damage today comes only from an undefended building being sieged directly (no wall, no garrison) — the fuller defense-in-depth cascade (wall→legacy wall→garrison→building) is real end-to-end via the other Phase 4/5 systems, this is just the last-resort case. ## 🎨 Phase 6.3: Art Asset Pipeline — ✅ Infrastructure built; unit PNGs still not generated
**Terrain and buildings ended up hand-authored SVG**, not AI-generated — a mid-project pivot once it was clear no image-generation tool exists in this environment for a first pass, and the user directly authored/approved the SVG route for these two categories. 11 terrain tiles (one per biome/soil-fertility combo) and 18 building sprites, both via lazy-load/cache/`ResourceLoader.exists()`-gated-null `*Visuals.gd` lookups (`TerrainVisuals`/`BuildingVisuals`) so art lands incrementally with zero further code changes. **Real bug found and fixed:** `Polygon2D` UV sampling only reads texture data at each of a hex's 6 corner vertices, not per-pixel — Strategic-zoom ground rendered as a near-flat blob until `GroundMode`/`corner_uvs()` was removed in favor of the same true per-pixel tiling Tactical view always used correctly.

The pipeline was extended the same way to five more categories (buildings retrofit with a second distinctiveness-focused prompt pass, zombies, resource/HUD icons — deliberately NOT bird's-eye at icon scale, a reasoned departure — walls as tileable strip textures, and props), all following the identical additive pattern.
